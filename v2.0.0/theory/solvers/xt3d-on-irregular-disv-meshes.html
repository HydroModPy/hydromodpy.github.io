XT3D on irregular meshes#

The two-point flux of (19) is exact when the line between two cell centers is perpendicular to the shared face and the conductivity is the same in every horizontal direction. On irregular cells it is not. XT3D [Provost et al., 2017] is the correction MODFLOW 6 offers, and this page gives the rule that turns it on. The angle of a connection is defined on 5. Flow between two cells, and the angle.

The error of the two-point flux#

For an isotropic conductivity, the flux through the face shared by cells \(n\) and \(m\) is \(\mathbf{n}_{nm}\cdot\mathbf{q} = -K\,\mathbf{n}_{nm}\cdot\nabla h\). Write the unit vector of the connection, \(\hat{\mathbf d}_{nm} = \mathbf{d}_{nm} / \lVert\mathbf{d}_{nm}\rVert\), as \(\cos\varphi_{nm}\,\mathbf{n}_{nm} + \sin\varphi_{nm}\,\mathbf{t}_{nm}\), where \(\mathbf{t}_{nm}\) is the unit vector along the face. Then

(29)#\[\mathbf{n}_{nm}\cdot\nabla h = \frac{\hat{\mathbf d}_{nm}\cdot\nabla h}{\cos\varphi_{nm}} - \tan\varphi_{nm}\;\mathbf{t}_{nm}\cdot\nabla h .\]

The heads \(h_n\) and \(h_m\) give the first term only. The conductance \(G_{nm}\) keeps it and drops the second, the head gradient along the face times \(\tan\varphi_{nm}\). This share is zero on an orthogonal connection, 0.27 of the tangential gradient at 15 degrees, 0.58 at 30 degrees and 1 at 45 degrees. A conductivity with two different horizontal values adds a similar term where its axes do not follow the connections, even on an orthogonal grid. The vertical \(K_z\) follows the layers and needs no XT3D.

What XT3D computes#

XT3D writes the flux through each face with the full head gradient, estimated from the heads of both cells and of their neighbors. The tangential term is then in the balance. The price is a wider stencil and a matrix that is not symmetric, so HydroModPy promotes a SIMPLE IMS preset to COMPLEX (The solution: Newton and IMS). On the validation case dupuit_uniform_recharge_1d run on irregular triangles, the head RMSE falls from 0.211 m to 0.0077 m, a factor of 27.

The two tensor rows come first (The conductivity tensor), because their error does not shrink with refinement. Take a steady well with \(K_{22} = K/10\) and angle1 at 30 degrees on a structured grid. The head error is 2.2 m for a 7 m drawdown, with cells of 40 m and of 20 m. With XT3D it is 0.04 m, then 0.013 m. XT3D costs from about 1.5 times the MODFLOW 6 run time on one layer to about 10 times on ten layers, and the RuntimeWarning says so. The code also writes the warning to the log. Setting mf6_enable_xt3d = true keeps XT3D on without the warning. The triangle and non-orthogonality rows only log at INFO level, with no warning.

The Voronoi cells of the voronoi engine are orthogonal by construction (4. The Voronoi engine), so XT3D stays off there when K22 equals \(K\).

The same case on two grids#

Assumptions#

  • The conductivity is isotropic in the horizontal plane unless the flow sets K22 or the angles.

  • The code measures the angle of a connection in the plan. The layers are an extrusion, so the vertical connections are orthogonal.

Limits#

  • XT3D exists in MODFLOW 6 only. MODFLOW-NWT runs on structured grids, and the Boussinesq solver has no correction for skewed cells.

  • Any K22 on an unstructured mesh turns XT3D on, even if the connections happen to follow the axes of the tensor.

  • Every run of a sweep near the trigger pays for the wider stencil.

  • On triangle cells under Newton, XT3D can cycle without converging. MODFLOW 6 scales the XT3D face area by the saturation of the upstream cell, but the cross term of XT3D is not zero at equal heads, so the discrete system can have no root. The steady state of the Nançon on triangles stops with E401 this way, in MODFLOW 6.6.3 and 6.8.1, and ghost nodes (GNC) do not converge either. The build warns about it. The voronoi engine needs no XT3D and converges on the same inputs. With mf6_enable_xt3d = false the run converges too, with heads about 0.1 m RMSE away from XT3D.

Path
solver.modflow6.runtime.mf6_enable_xt3d
More

When left to None, XT3D turns on where a consistent result needs it, with a RuntimeWarning: a tilted K tensor (a non-zero flow parameter angle2 or angle3, which MF6 reads only under XT3D), and a K22 different from K on a cell whose angle1 is not a multiple of 90 degrees, or anywhere on an unstructured mesh. Without XT3D the two-point flux drops the cross terms of such a tensor, and the head error does not shrink with refinement. XT3D also turns on, with an INFO log, on triangle cells and on a significantly non-orthogonal mesh. A structured grid with K along its axes, or an orthogonal mesh with K22 equal to K, keeps it off. XT3D costs from about 1.5 times the MF6 run time on one layer to about 10 times on ten layers. true turns it on without a warning; false turns it off, with a RuntimeWarning when a consistent result needs it.
