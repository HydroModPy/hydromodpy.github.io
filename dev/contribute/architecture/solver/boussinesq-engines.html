Boussinesq engines and experimental closures#

The equations of the Boussinesq solver are on Boussinesq theory. This page is for the contributor who works on how they are solved: the method and engine catalogs, each engine step by step, the two experimental obstacle closures, and the diagnostics a run writes. The module diagrams are on Boussinesq solver diagrams.

Methods and engines#

Two choices are independent. The method is the physics: the unknowns, the closure, the space scheme and the time scheme. The engine is the numerics: the nonlinear solver, the matrix layout, the Jacobian and the linear solver. Two runs can share a method and differ by the engine. The keys are flow.surface_interaction_model for the closure and flow.runtime_backend for the engine.

The PETSc engines run only on Linux and need petsc4py: on another system the run stops with a message that starts Boussinesq runtime_backend='petsc' is only supported on Linux. They run in serial, on PETSc.COMM_SELF.

The nonlinear solve#

Every engine looks for a head \(h\) such that the residual vector is zero. The vector it tests is the solver residual: the balance in m³/s on a free cell, and \(h_j - H^D_j\) in metres on a constrained cell. The tolerance flow.runtime_tol_residual_inf applies to the largest absolute entry. The iteration budget is flow.runtime_max_iterations; when the key is not set, the runtime has its own budget, larger on scipy_sparse than on the others (build_runtime_options in solver_contract.py). A step that does not converge ends the run.

The local engine#

The local engine is a damped Newton loop (_newton_loop_template in runtimes/newton_common.py), written to be read:

  1. start from the initial guess and assemble the residual;

  2. build a dense Jacobian by forward finite differences, with a step of \(10^{-7}\max(1, \lvert h \rvert)\) on each head in turn (build_dense_fd_jacobian);

  3. solve \(J\,\delta = -R\) with a dense solve;

  4. halve the step, from 1 down to a minimum factor, until the largest residual entry falls; if it never does, the solve stops and is not converged;

  5. stop when the largest residual entry is below the tolerance.

The method is transparent and fit for small validation meshes. A dense engine assembles a dense Jacobian, so an explicit local or scipy refuses a mesh of more than 400 cells (_assert_runtime_mesh_size_supported in boussinesq.py). The default auto runs local up to 400 cells and scipy_sparse above, and logs the engine once (resolve_runtime_backend_name in solver_contract.py).

The SciPy engine#

The scipy engine keeps the same residual. It gives SciPy a semi-analytic dense Jacobian and lets scipy.optimize.root with the hybr method drive the solve (runtimes/scipy_dense.py). SciPy stops on its own criterion on the step, set from flow.runtime_tol_state_update_inf, and the run then checks the residual against the tolerance: a solve that SciPy calls successful but whose residual is above the tolerance is reported as not converged. It has the same limit of 400 cells.

The SciPy sparse engine#

The scipy_sparse engine runs the same Newton loop as local with a sparse Jacobian (runtimes/scipy_sparse.py):

  1. the smooth terms, which are the internal fluxes, the storage, the drain and the constraints, are differentiated analytically;

  2. the release \(s_i\) is completed by finite differences, with columns of the matrix that do not share a row perturbed together (graph colouring);

  3. the Newton step is solved with spsolve.

If the matrix is singular, the step is solved again with a diagonal shift that grows by a factor of ten at each of up to six tries. For a steady solve the initial guess is moved to the middle of the cell wherever the head sits exactly on the top or the bottom, because the partition Jacobian can be singular there (interiorize_regularized_partition_initial_guess). This is the reference engine for larger meshes on every system.

The PETSc engines#

The PETSc partition engine solves the same head-only residual with PETSc SNES, a Newton method with a backtracking line search, on a sparse semi-analytic Jacobian (runtimes/petsc_partition.py). The linear systems are solved by a direct LU factorisation with a small shift of the diagonal against zero pivots (_configure_default_snes in runtimes/petsc_common.py). The absolute tolerance of SNES is the residual tolerance, and its relative and step tolerances are off. The Jacobian also receives a diagonal shift that decays with the residual, a light pseudo-transient regularisation for large meshes:

\[ \mathrm{shift} = \max\Bigl(10^{-8},\ 0.2\;\mathrm{median}_{J_{ii} \neq 0}\, \lvert J_{ii}\rvert\ \min\bigl(1,\ \lVert R\rVert_\infty / \lVert R_0\rVert_\infty\bigr)\Bigr) \]

where \(R_0\) is the residual of the first iterate. The shift vanishes as the solve converges. The residual does not contain it, so the solution is the one of the unshifted system (regularized_partition_jacobian_shift in runtimes/partition_utils.py).

The mixed engine solves the system of \(3n\) unknowns of The lower obstacle, with the rates scaled by \(q_s\) (_solve_mixed_problem in runtimes/petsc_mixed.py). The residual has three blocks: the balance with \(-a_i q^{\mathrm{dry}}_i\), the Fischer-Burmeister equation of the surface, and the Fischer-Burmeister equation of the bottom. The run converges when SNES reports a convergence and the largest entry of the full residual is below the tolerance.

The starting point of the mixed solve is a numerical choice, not a physical one. The head is clipped between the bottom and the top. A free cell whose balance at the bottom still asks to lose water starts at the bottom. In a transient run, the first guess of \(q^{\mathrm{ex}}\) is the release the regularised partition would give when the recharge is positive or a well flux is negative, and zero otherwise (_initial_transient_q_ex_guess). That guess uses a depth scale of 5 % of the head scale \(h_s\), not flow.partition_release_depth_m, so that it starts off the kink of the Fischer-Burmeister function. The choice changes the path to the solution, not the equations.

The VI engines solve the bounded system of The experimental obstacle closures.

The experimental obstacle closures#

Two more closures exist and are marked experimental on their cards. Both run on PETSc only, and both keep the head as the only unknown.

  • vi_obstacle solves the balance with the head bounded between the bottom and the land surface, as a variational inequality (PETSc SNESVI). The release is not in the system: \(s_i = 0\) while the solver iterates. After convergence, the balance left on a cell at its upper bound is read as the saturation excess, and on a cell at its lower bound as the unmet demand (reconstruct_obstacle_reactions in runtimes/petsc_vi/obstacle.py):

    \[ q^{\mathrm{ex}}_i = \frac{\max(-R_i,\ 0)}{a_i}, \qquad q^{\mathrm{dry}}_i = \frac{\max(R_i,\ 0)}{a_i} \]

    The first expression is used on a cell at its upper bound and the second on a cell at its lower bound. The upper bound is relaxed when a positive drain conductance is declared, so that the drain carries the exchange at the surface (runtimes/vi_bounds.py). Each time step can be cut into substeps, with flow.vi_substeps_per_period, and a failed time step can be retried with more substeps.

  • ts_vi_obstacle hands the time stepping to PETSc TS with a backward Euler scheme, inside each time step, and keeps the same bounds. The storage term is \(a_i S_{y,i}\,\dot h_i\), because the head is bounded. The number of steps and the adaptivity are set by the ts_vi_* keys listed below.

A steady solve for the initial state of a ts_vi_obstacle run uses the regularised partition (steady_state_initialization_surface_interaction_model in solver/steady_initial_conditions.py).

A file that chooses the experimental closure and cuts each period into substeps:

base_config = "step4_transient.toml"

[solver]
backend = { backend = "boussinesq" }

[[simulation.process]]
id = "flow_main"
type = "flow"
solvers = ["boussinesq"]

# The rivers the mesh follows: every cell that drains more than 0.5 km2.
[geographic.river_network]
enabled = true
threshold_area_km2 = 0.5

[mesh_catchment]
constraints_mode = "rivers_only"
cache = true

[flow]

vi_substep_on_failure = true

What the run reports#

The run writes _boussinesq_state_history.npz and _boussinesq_summary.json in the solver folder. The summary carries diagnostics of the surface and of the bottom, computed on the accepted snapshots (runtime_summary.py):

Key family

Meaning

surface_threshold_*

Cells where the surface release is active, the number of windows in which it turns on and off, the peak rate and the peak head above the surface.

bottom_threshold_*

Accepted heads below the bottom of the aquifer, and the negative storage volume they would imply.

bottom_constraint_*

Cells where the dry deficit is active, its peak rate and its integrated volume.

dry_deficit_history_m_s

The state history of \(q^{\mathrm{dry}}\), in the npz file.

Read the dry deficit apart from the discharge terms. It says where the bottom constraint was active and how much demand the storage could not meet.
