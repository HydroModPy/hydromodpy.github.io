The solvers of the aquifer#

HydroModPy integrates the groundwater flow equation with one of three solvers: MODFLOW 6, MODFLOW-NWT or the in-house Boussinesq solver. This page says which packages and options HydroModPy writes for each of them, how it sets the time steps, what happens to a cell that dries, how the two MODFLOW solvers differ, and how a transport run hooks onto a flow run. What each solver supports is listed in Solvers.

Note

Scope. The balance of a cell, (19), is on The groundwater flow problem. The Boussinesq solver is on Boussinesq theory and the XT3D terms on XT3D on irregular meshes.

Sources#

The MODFLOW equations, packages and numerical methods are documented by the USGS. This page does not repeat them. It says which options HydroModPy writes.

  • MODFLOW 6 groundwater flow model: control-volume formulation, Newton-Raphson, storage and stress packages [Langevin et al., 2017].

  • MODFLOW 6 framework and the IMS linear solver [Hughes et al., 2017].

  • XT3D option of the NPF package [Provost et al., 2017].

  • MODFLOW-NWT, the Newton formulation of MODFLOW-2005 [Niswonger et al., 2011], on the MODFLOW-2005 groundwater flow process [Harbaugh, 2005].

  • MODFLOW 6 groundwater transport model [Langevin et al., 2022] and water mover package [Morway et al., 2021].

  • The MODFLOW 6 input guide and the MODFLOW-NWT guide, for the definition of each package and option.

Time steps#

The three solvers read the same time grid, built from simulation.time. Each stress period has a length \(\Delta t\) and holds every forcing constant. The two MODFLOW solvers cut it into \(n_\mathrm{sub}\) equal steps, \(\delta t = \Delta t / n_\mathrm{sub}\), with a step multiplier of 1: MODFLOW 6 in its TDIS package, MODFLOW-NWT in DIS.

Equal steps are a choice with a test behind it. On a confined column against a closed-form solution, test_stress_period_substepping finds equal steps more accurate than a geometric growth of the steps, in either direction, and one step per period about ten times less accurate than ten steps (Validation). The unconfined problem is nonlinear, so this holds there only approximately. The same case reports that one step per period moves the monthly discharge of a drained hillslope by tens of per cent against a refined run. One step per period is therefore not a neutral choice.

A period is steady or transient. With flow.flow_regime set to "steady", every period is steady. With "transient", only the first period is steady, and only when flow.first_period_steady is true. With solver.modflow6.runtime.mf6_ats, MODFLOW 6 adds adaptive time stepping to the transient periods: each period starts at the step above, ATS subdivides it only where the solver cannot converge, and it never grows past that step. Steady periods are left out. The starting heads are on The starting heads. In the code: build_temporal_discretization_from_time_grid.

The nonlinear solve#

MODFLOW 6 and MODFLOW-NWT both write the balance (19) for every active cell and every step, and solve the nonlinear system by Newton-Raphson outer iterations, with a linear solve inside each one [Hughes et al., 2017, Niswonger et al., 2011]. The tests that stop the iteration and the percent discrepancy of the budget are the ones of the listing file, defined in the sources above. They do not measure the same thing on the two solvers: MODFLOW-NWT tests a norm of the residual over all cells, MODFLOW 6 a maximum over the cells of the linear solve. What HydroModPy adds:

  • The keys that set the tests are solver.modflow6.runtime.mf6_outer_dvclose, solver.modflow6.runtime.mf6_inner_dvclose and solver.modflow6.runtime.mf6_inner_rclose for MODFLOW 6, and solver.modflow_nwt.runtime.nwt.headtol and solver.modflow_nwt.runtime.nwt.fluxtol for MODFLOW-NWT. Heads are in m, flows in m³/s. HydroModPy writes inner_rclose with no option, which keeps the absolute largest residual of a cell. When the key is unset, the value of the complexity preset applies.

  • The run metrics keep the percent discrepancy of the last step and the worst one of the run, both read from the volume budget of the entire model and not from a package budget. A calibration rejects a trial whose worst step is above calibration.reject_water_budget_above (one per cent unless the file says otherwise). The auxiliary steady solve of a steady_state start accepts its result only within 0.1 per cent. In the code: model_percent_discrepancies.

The grid#

MODFLOW 6 reads the cells of every mesh engine as a DISV grid: one code path covers the three. A regular model is a DISV grid whose cells are the DEM pixels. The cells of the voronoi engine are orthogonal to their connections, and the triangles of the triangle engine are not, which turns XT3D on. The engine is mesh.engine and the number of layers is domain.layers.nlay, read from the table [domain.layers] that MODFLOW-NWT reads too. The mesh is the subject of The mesh.

The active cell of a column. idomain is 1 for an active cell, 0 for an inactive one and -1 for a vertical pass-through cell, and every test of activity reads it as above 0. The packages that act on the surface of a column act on its top active cell, and not on the first layer: the drains, the supports of the constant heads, the surface of the evapotranspiration, the computational top below, the filling of the closed depressions, the lake footprint and the saturated thickness the calibration reads. The footprint of a lake stays closed to them. A constant head is written on active cells only.

The computational top. Under Newton, MODFLOW 6 builds the horizontal conductance from the full thickness of the two cells times the saturation of the upstream cell, where MODFLOW-NWT and MODFLOW-USG use the saturated thickness (GwfConductanceUtils.f90, NPF) [Langevin et al., 2017]. Where the thickness \(H\) changes from one cell to the next, the transmissivity is then too low by about \(\Delta H / (2H)\). The grid MODFLOW 6 reads therefore has a computational top \(z'_\mathrm{top} = z_\mathrm{top} + \max H_1 - H_1\), with \(H_1\) the thickness of the top layer under the ground: every cell of the top layer gets the same thickness. A thickness that is the same everywhere gives \(z_\mathrm{top}\) back, so no file changes then. The top moves only on the columns whose top active cell is in the top layer: a column with a pinched first layer keeps its interfaces, since its active cell joins cells of the second layer, which cannot be raised. This is a numerical device of HydroModPy, not a USGS practice. Every package elevation (drains, EVT, streambeds, lake banks, barriers, the ocean), the initial heads and the run store keep the ground. Water above the ground stays in the aquifer up to \(z'_\mathrm{top}\), with its specific yield, where a cell topped at the ground would turn confined; the drains at the ground keep that rise to a few centimeters. Two limits. A lake column keeps its ground, since a raised top would reach into the lake, so the thickness still changes at the banks. Only the top of the top layer moves: a lower layer whose saturated part changes from cell to cell keeps the bias. Anyone who opens the native MODFLOW 6 files (flopy, ModelMuse) sees a top above the ground wherever the thickness varies. That is intended; the ground is topography in the run store. In the code: disv_grid_kwargs.

Flow between cells, and XT3D#

NPF receives \(K\), \(K_z = K/\beta\) and the convertible flag, and K22 and the three angles when the flow sets them (The conductivity tensor). With the two-point conductance (20), the flux between two cells is exact only when the line between the centers is perpendicular to the shared face and the conductivity is isotropic. XT3D adds the missing terms [Provost et al., 2017]. HydroModPy decides about it once per run; the rule and its trigger are on XT3D on irregular meshes. XT3D makes the matrix non-symmetric, so the IMS preset is promoted when needed (The solution: Newton and IMS). Two tensors need XT3D for a consistent result, so HydroModPy turns it on and raises a RuntimeWarning on its cost. The first is a tilted tensor (angle2 or angle3 not zero), because MODFLOW 6 reads these two angles only under XT3D. A tilt writes the three angles, the missing ones as zero, since MODFLOW 6 refuses ANGLE2 without ANGLE3. The second is a K22 different from \(K\) on a cell whose angle1 is not a multiple of 90 degrees, or on any cell of an unstructured mesh. The axes of the tensor then leave the connections and the two-point flux drops the cross terms [Provost et al., 2017], so the head error does not shrink with refinement. For a steady well with \(K_{22} = K/10\) and angle1 at 30 degrees on a structured grid, the head RMSE stayed at 2.2 m for a 7 m drawdown with cells of 40 m then 20 m, and fell to 0.04 then 0.01 m under XT3D. XT3D costs time. On a steady benchmark of 120 by 120 cells and 10 layers, a run took 115 to 136 s with it, against 12 s without. A written solver.modflow6.runtime.mf6_enable_xt3d is still obeyed, true without a warning and false with a RuntimeWarning when one of these two tensors is set. In the code: resolve_xt3d_decision, k_tensor_is_tilted, k_tensor_is_off_axis.

Evaporation, drains, lakes and streams#

The laws, conductances and worked cells of these exits are on How water leaves the aquifer.

  • Evaporation. Under MODFLOW 6, EVTA receives a routed negative recharge (flow.sinks_sources.recharge.negative_to_evt) or, in soil mode, the ETP the soil could not meet, with the layer array IEVT when the layers hold a pass-through cell. The loaded ETP is ignored outside soil mode, with a warning (Drains and evapotranspiration at the top of the model).

  • Drains. One drain in the top active layer of every column that is not an ocean or stream constant-head cell. It sits at the ground surface or half a band depth below it. solver.sink_fill sets it to zero in closed depressions (Drains and evapotranspiration at the top of the model). In the code: build_drain_stress_period_data.

  • Lakes, streams and barriers. LAK, SFR, MVR and HFB exist under MODFLOW 6 only. LAK and SFR are built after the drains, so that the drains that coincide with a stream reach can be removed and the drainage of the hillslope can be handed to the stream or lake it flows to. MVR is built last, because it names the packages it moves water between (Lakes and reservoirs, Streams and the water mover).

Imposed heads and wells#

CHD imposes the head of the ocean, stream and lateral boundaries on their cells. MODFLOW 6 takes one CHD package, so the three supports are merged, and a cell claimed twice keeps the head written last. WEL adds the rate of each well. Under the Newton formulation the code sets auto_flow_reduce on WEL, so that a well that dewaters its cell tapers its extraction instead of making the head oscillate across the threshold. MODFLOW 6 reads it as a fraction of the cell height, and the option is one value per package: it is the smallest fraction that keeps the interval within a tenth of the aquifer below the ground in every well cell, 0.1 when the top is the ground. In the code: assemble_constant_head_stress_period_data, well_flow_reduce_fraction.

The solution: Newton and IMS#

The code writes NEWTON, and adds UNDER_RELAXATION when solver.modflow6.runtime.mf6_newton_under_relaxation is true. Newton is on by default, because every layer is convertible. The UNDER_RELAXATION of the Newton option damps the head of a cell that Newton would push below its bottom. It is not the under-relaxation of IMS, which adjusts the update factor of the outer iteration.

The code passes the closure criteria, the iteration limits and the optional solver.modflow6.runtime.mf6_linear_acceleration, solver.modflow6.runtime.mf6_under_relaxation and solver.modflow6.runtime.mf6_inner_rclose to the IMS package, with the optional solver.modflow6.runtime.mf6_reordering_method. The IMS can renumber the cells before it factorises, with reverse Cuthill-McKee (rcm) or minimum degree (md) [Hughes et al., 2017]. The default, none, keeps the numbering of the mesh and writes nothing. The cases measured on 2026-10-26 gave no gain: on Voronoi meshes of 14 400 to 144 000 cells, in a shuffled or a row-sorted numbering, and on the steady Flume solve of 73 400 cells, rcm and md added 3 to 73 per cent of inner iterations and did not cut the wall time. The option reaches the IMS of the flow and the one of the transport. The complexity, solver.modflow6.runtime.mf6_ims_complexity, defaults to COMPLEX. A configured SIMPLE complexity is promoted to COMPLEX when XT3D is on, and to at least MODERATE under Newton, because SIMPLE assumes a symmetric, well-conditioned matrix. Two combinations are refused:

  • solver.modflow6.runtime.mf6_newton and solver.modflow6.runtime.mf6_enable_rewet together stop the run with [HMPY.E405] when the NPF package is built, because MODFLOW 6 forbids rewetting under the Newton formulation.

  • Conjugate-gradient acceleration under Newton stops with [HMPY.E406], because the Newton Jacobian is not symmetric and CG cannot solve it. The check runs before any grid is built. Use BICGSTAB, or leave solver.modflow6.runtime.mf6_linear_acceleration unset.

In the code: resolve_ims_complexity.

The static ocean and side boundaries are not CHD rows. They are cells with a negative ibound in BAS, which imposes the starting head on them. Four things differ from MODFLOW 6.

  • The mesh must be the regular engine. A file whose mesh.engine is another engine is refused when it is read. NWT builds the grid of the DEM pixels, or a coarser one given by mesh.cell_size.

  • The unconfined law is the upstream-weighted UPW package [Niswonger et al., 2011], with a smoothing of the saturated thickness set by solver.modflow_nwt.runtime.nwt.thickfact.

  • EVT uses the topography. The code sets the EVT surface to the top lowered by flow.sinks_sources.etp.surface_offset and the extinction depth to flow.sinks_sources.etp.extinction_depth, with nevtop from solver.modflow_nwt.runtime.evt.nevtop.   • No soil reservoir, lakes, streams or barriers. The checks listed under Limits stop the file.

In the code: assemble_flopy_packages.

Wetting and drying#

A cell dries when the head falls to its bottom. Under Newton, MODFLOW 6 smooths the saturated thickness near the bottom with a fraction of the thickness that it sets internally, and HydroModPy has no key for it [Langevin et al., 2017]. MODFLOW-NWT does the same in its SAT_THICK function, with the fraction read from thickfact [Niswonger et al., 2011]. The smoothing keeps the Newton step defined. It does not move the water table. The solvers differ in what they do next.

MODFLOW 6 against MODFLOW-NWT#

The same project file can run on either. The solver that runs is the first name in simulation.process.solvers of the flow process. solver.backend is the fallback when no process names one, and the key the compatibility checks of a file are run against. Write the same name in both. solver.backend is a plain string, the name of a registered flow solver, and an unknown name is refused with the names this installation serves. The settings of each solver are a table under its name, [solver.modflow_nwt] and [solver.modflow6]. This page ranks neither solver.

The vertical conductivity is a ratio on both, \(K_z = K / \beta\). The key is solver.modflow_nwt.process_specific.vka for MODFLOW-NWT, with layvka set so that vka is read as a ratio, and solver.modflow6.process_specific.vka for MODFLOW 6. A Kv of the flow replaces the ratio on both. MODFLOW-NWT then writes layvka = 0 and vka = Kv, so UPW reads a conductivity, and a vka other than 1 beside a Kv is refused.

Sources and sinks. Recharge, wells, drains and constant heads are the same quantities in both. Two points differ.

  • The loaded ETP is a sink in MODFLOW-NWT, with its surface at the top minus flow.sinks_sources.etp.surface_offset and the extinction depth flow.sinks_sources.etp.extinction_depth. MODFLOW 6 draws none outside soil mode, and says so. In soil mode it writes the ETP the soil left unmet (The daily soil reservoir).

  • A negative recharge sent to EVT by flow.sinks_sources.recharge.negative_to_evt follows the same rule in both (Rain and evaporation: the forcing), with a different geometry: the model top and solver.modflow6.process_specific.evt_extinction_depth in MODFLOW 6, the two etp keys above or fixed constants in MODFLOW-NWT.

Comparing the two. To compare the solvers, set the head test to the same value on both sides (solver.modflow_nwt.runtime.nwt.headtol against the two dvclose keys): the residual tests do not measure the same thing. Validation lists the cases that run both solvers against the same closed-form solution, and runs both on the catchment of example 04, where their heads differ by less than a millimeter. Where the cell thickness changes from one cell to the next, the two conductances differ, and the computational top brings MODFLOW 6 back to MODFLOW-NWT: on the circular-island cases, whose top follows the ground over a flat bottom, both sit at the same distance from the closed form. Validation gives the thresholds and the reason for each. A threshold is a bound a test accepts, not a measured error, so it ranks nothing.

The Boussinesq solver#

The in-house solver writes one residual per triangle of the triangle engine. Its time steps and the choice of its engine (solver.boussinesq.backend) are on Boussinesq theory. solver.boussinesq.surface_interaction_model picks the closure for the water that reaches the surface. It builds recharge, wells, the side, stream and ocean imposed heads, and top drainage, and refuses the rest. Its flux between two cells uses the two cell centers only: on triangles far from equilateral it is not consistent, and the patch test of Validation shows the error that refinement does not remove. Its equations and closures are on Boussinesq theory.

The linear reservoir#

The sixth solver replaces the grid of the aquifer with one state: the depth of water on the cell. It integrates ds/dt = R - s/tau exactly on every piece of constant rate the run carries, where R is the recharge of the run and tau the time constant of [solver.linear_reservoir]. One discharge and one reservoir storage leave the solve for every period, at the station of the whole catchment, and the budget rows of the terms recharge, drain and storage close it on the mass by construction.

Transport coupling#

A transport process attaches to the flow process that came before it. The planner binds each transport run to an earlier flow run of the right solver, in the order written in the file, and stops with requires an earlier process using flow/<solver> when there is none. The pairs are declared by each adapter as its requires attribute.

The adapter of a transport receives four objects from the runner. model is the frozen Model the flow was built from, the only Model a transport works from. options is the validated table of its solver, [transport.modflow6] for the GWT of MODFLOW 6. The folder it works in is the folder of the flow run itself, and upstream[0] is the SolverRun of that flow.

What a transport declares as its reads is face_flow: the flux across each connection between two cells, at every time step, in the format the solver files of the flow carry. The name is the one of the field registry, where connection_cells sits beside it and names the two cells of each connection, the smaller flat index first. The run store keeps the pair only when simulation.results.keep_face_flows is true, in m3/s, one value per connection at each stored stamp, the mean of the steps of the stamp weighted by their length. A run of the default keeps no inter-cell flow, and the diagonal of FLOW-JA-FACE, the residual of each cell, is never kept, because the per-cell budget already carries it. MODFLOW 6 writes it as FLOW-JA-FACE in the cell-by-cell budget file of every step; MODFLOW-NWT writes its face flows in the same file, and the link file of MT3DMS reads from it. The flow solver writes what reads needs: a file that pairs a transport with a flow solver that writes none of it is refused when the file is read, with the flow solvers that write it. A transport of the family of its flow reaches what it reads through that flow run, and none of them reads the run store during the solve. A concentration transport stores its answer under the name of the solute, concentration/<spc_name> in the run store, and a store written by a 2.0.0 run still reads from the bare path.

MODFLOW 6 GWT#

Modflow6Transport adds a GWT model to the MODFLOW 6 simulation of the flow run [Langevin et al., 2022]. It reuses the DISV grid and the idomain of the flow model, couples the two models with a GWF6-GWT6 exchange, writes the whole simulation again and solves it: the flow is then solved twice, in the same folder. It gives GWT its own IMS with BICGSTAB, because advection makes the matrix non-symmetric. The transport solve is linear, so it uses the configured complexity and no Newton promotion. What HydroModPy sets in the GWT model:

  • advection with the scheme of transport.modflow6.scheme;

  • dispersion only when transport.modflow6.disp_long or transport.modflow6.diffu_coeff is above zero;

  • the mobile storage with transport.modflow6.porosity. Without it, the code falls back on the specific yield and warns that this underestimates the pore volume, because the pore velocity is the Darcy flux divided by the porosity;

  • decay of order zero or one, from transport.modflow6.react_order and transport.modflow6.rate_decay.

Recharge is the only source of solute. Its concentration travels as the CONCENTRATION auxiliary variable of RCHA, and the source and sink mixing package reads it. Constant-head cells and wells bring water in at concentration zero.

MODFLOW 6 particle tracking#

modflow6_prt builds a PRT model on the same flow model. It needs the cell-by-cell velocity that solver.modflow6.runtime.save_specific_discharge writes. Its keys are in [transport].

MODFLOW-NWT: MODPATH and MT3DMS#

MODPATH builds a MODPATH 6 input from the NWT run and stops if the flow model is not MODFLOW-NWT. MT3DMS reads the link file that the LMT package writes, and NWT adds LMT when a transport run follows it: the run declares face_flow through its reads, which the runner collects into the hand_off member of the slot of the flow run. The file name is solver.modflow_nwt.runtime.lmt.output_file_name. In the code: hand_off, filled by slot_for of the runner.

MODPATH releases its particles from the seepage areas of the flow run when transport.modpath.zone_partic is "seepage_clip". The mask is built from the heads of the flow run itself, the water table of the first stored step against the topography at half a drain band depth, exactly the rule the run store derives for its seepage_mask, and the store is never read during the solve.

Assumptions#

  • Flow is saturated, in one phase, and the head is the only unknown. Water above the water table is not modeled: the code writes no UZF package, and the soil reservoir of The daily soil reservoir is a separate daily model that hands a recharge to the solver.

  • Every layer is convertible: it can be confined or unconfined from one step to the next.

  • The horizontal conductivity is isotropic, unless the flow sets K22 or the angles under MODFLOW 6. The vertical one is the horizontal one divided by one ratio for the whole model, or a per-cell \(K_z\) field.

Limits#

The matrix of what each solver declares is generated on Solvers. One rule gives most of the refusals. Each flow solver lists in supports the traits of the model it honours, and a file is refused when it is read if it uses a trait that the solver of the run leaves out. The message gives the key the file wrote, the solver chosen and the solvers that honour the trait, for example solver.backend = 'modflow_nwt' does not honor flow.sinks_sources.lakes: it would be ignored without a word. Remove it, or run a flow solver that honors it (modflow6). A solver added by a plugin is checked by the same rule. A run sealed on disk is read back with a warning instead of a refusal. The refusals are these.

  • Mesh. MODFLOW 6 reads every engine, MODFLOW-NWT the regular engine and Boussinesq the triangle engine. A file that pairs a solver with another engine is refused when it is read: the message starts solver 'modflow_nwt' reads the meshes of the engines. The size limit of the dense Boussinesq engines is on Boussinesq theory.

  • Boundaries. Lakes, reservoirs, SFR and barriers need MODFLOW 6. The stream boundary needs MODFLOW 6 or Boussinesq. With solver.backend at modflow_nwt, the file is refused when it is read if it declares a stream network, a lake or a stream boundary, flow barriers or a dam cutoff wall. The message names the table the file wrote, for instance flow.sinks_sources.sfr for a stream network.

  • Layers and tensor. MODFLOW-NWT has no pass-through cell: pinch_out = "pass_through" is refused when the file is read, and the build refuses a grid that holds one. K22 and the angles need MODFLOW 6, and the file is refused with MODFLOW-NWT or Boussinesq. An angle2 outside \([-90, 90]\) degrees and an angle with a vertical profile are refused. Boussinesq solves one layer and reads no domain.layers, so it refuses more than one layer and the surfaces mode. A vertical profile of K under a solver that lists no layers only warns, since the solver reads one K per column.

  • Soil reservoir. [flow.sinks_sources.recharge.soil] is refused with every solver that does not list soil, which is MODFLOW 6 alone today.

  • ETP. MODFLOW 6 draws no ETP outside soil mode, and only logs a warning. Boussinesq lists no etp and refuses it when the file is read.

  • Options of [solver]. solver.sink_fill, solver.drain_band_depth_m and solver.drain_conductance_floor_m2_s, set away from their defaults, are refused under Boussinesq. It reads none of them, and a value it ignores would still move the seepage mask that a figure derives from the stored band.

  • Transport. A transport solver follows the flow solvers its requires names. It is refused when solver.backend names another flow solver, with the flow solvers it follows. It is refused too when the flow solver of the file writes none of what the transport reads, face_flow, with the flow solvers that write it.

  • Hotstart and cyclic spin-up. flow.restart_from and a cyclic spin-up need MODFLOW 6, the only solver that lists the two traits in supports. A restart beside a steady or a cyclic start is refused for every solver, because the computed start would overwrite the restart.

  • Lake and cyclic spin-up. A lake with a spinup_cyclic start runs on MODFLOW 6. Each cycle starts from the final lake stages of the cycle before, the main run starts from those of the last cycle, and the loop settles only when the heads and the lake stages have both settled.

  • Windows. simulation.time.window_length is refused under a steady run, a transport, a calibration, and, for a chain of more than one window, a flow solver that does not read an earlier state, because window 2 starts from the final state of window 1.

  • A steady start that did not converge. The convergence of the auxiliary steady solve is known only at the solve. A transport run behind a steady start whose heads did not converge stops with SolverDivergedError, and says how to tighten the closure in solver.modflow6.runtime.mf6_outer_dvclose or to start from another state. Replaying a sealed run only warns.

  • Newton. Newton with rewet (HMPY.E405) and Newton with conjugate gradients (HMPY.E406) are refused (The solution: Newton and IMS).

  • Not represented. The code writes no unsaturated-zone package (UZF), no variable-density package (BUY), no subsidence package (CSUB), and no GHB or RIV package.
