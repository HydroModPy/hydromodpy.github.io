The solvers of the aquifer#

HydroModPy integrates the groundwater flow equation with one of three solvers: MODFLOW 6, MODFLOW-NWT or the in-house Boussinesq solver. This page says which packages and options HydroModPy writes for each of them, how it sets the time steps, what happens to a cell that dries, how the two MODFLOW solvers differ, and how a transport run hooks onto a flow run. What each solver supports is listed in Solvers.

Note

Scope. The balance of a cell, (17), is on The groundwater flow problem. The Boussinesq solver is on Boussinesq theory and the XT3D terms on XT3D on irregular meshes.

Sources#

The MODFLOW equations, packages and numerical methods are documented by the USGS. This page does not repeat them. It says which options HydroModPy writes.

  • MODFLOW 6 groundwater flow model: control-volume formulation, Newton-Raphson, storage and stress packages [Langevin et al., 2017].

  • MODFLOW 6 framework and the IMS linear solver [Hughes et al., 2017].

  • XT3D option of the NPF package [Provost et al., 2017].

  • MODFLOW-NWT, the Newton formulation of MODFLOW-2005 [Niswonger et al., 2011], on the MODFLOW-2005 groundwater flow process [Harbaugh, 2005].

  • MODFLOW 6 groundwater transport model [Langevin et al., 2022] and water mover package [Morway et al., 2021].

  • The MODFLOW 6 input guide and the MODFLOW-NWT guide, for the definition of each package and option.

Time steps#

The three solvers read the same time grid, built from simulation.time. Each stress period has a length \(\Delta t\) and holds every forcing constant. The two MODFLOW solvers cut it into \(n_\mathrm{sub}\) equal steps, \(\delta t = \Delta t / n_\mathrm{sub}\), with a step multiplier of 1: MODFLOW 6 in its TDIS package, MODFLOW-NWT in DIS.

Equal steps are a choice with a test behind it. On a confined column against a closed-form solution, test_stress_period_substepping finds equal steps more accurate than a geometric growth of the steps, in either direction, and one step per period about ten times less accurate than ten steps (Validation). The unconfined problem is nonlinear, so this holds there only approximately. The same case reports that one step per period moves the monthly discharge of a drained hillslope by tens of per cent against a refined run. One step per period is therefore not a neutral choice.

A period is steady or transient. With flow.flow_regime set to "steady", every period is steady. With "transient", only the first period is steady, and only when flow.first_period_steady is true. With modflow6.runtime.mf6_ats, MODFLOW 6 adds adaptive time stepping to the transient periods: each period starts at the step above, ATS subdivides it only where the solver cannot converge, and it never grows past that step. Steady periods are left out. The starting heads are on The starting heads. In the code: build_temporal_discretization_from_time_grid.

The nonlinear solve#

MODFLOW 6 and MODFLOW-NWT both write the balance (17) for every active cell and every step, and solve the nonlinear system by Newton-Raphson outer iterations, with a linear solve inside each one [Hughes et al., 2017, Niswonger et al., 2011]. The tests that stop the iteration and the percent discrepancy of the budget are the ones of the listing file, defined in the sources above. They do not measure the same thing on the two solvers: MODFLOW-NWT tests a norm of the residual over all cells, MODFLOW 6 a maximum over the cells of the linear solve. What HydroModPy adds:

  • The keys that set the tests are modflow6.runtime.mf6_outer_dvclose, modflow6.runtime.mf6_inner_dvclose and modflow6.runtime.mf6_inner_rclose for MODFLOW 6, and modflownwt.runtime.nwt.headtol and modflownwt.runtime.nwt.fluxtol for MODFLOW-NWT. Heads are in m, flows in m³/s. HydroModPy writes inner_rclose with no option, which keeps the absolute largest residual of a cell. When the key is unset, the value of the complexity preset applies.

  • The run metrics keep the percent discrepancy of the last step and the worst one of the run. A calibration rejects a trial whose last value is above calibration.reject_water_budget_above. The auxiliary steady solve of a steady_state start accepts its result only within 0.1 per cent. In the code: percent_discrepancies.

The grid#

MODFLOW 6 reads every grid as a DISV grid, structured or not: one code path covers both. A structured model is a DISV grid whose cells are the DEM pixels. A mesh built from Gmsh is, by default, the Voronoi dual of its triangles, which is orthogonal to its connections. modflow6.sgrid.grid_dual keeps the triangles as cells instead. The number of layers is modflow6.sgrid.vertical.nlay. The mesh is the subject of The mesh.

The computational top. Under Newton, MODFLOW 6 builds the horizontal conductance from the full thickness of the two cells times the saturation of the upstream cell, where MODFLOW-NWT and MODFLOW-USG use the saturated thickness (GwfConductanceUtils.f90, NPF) [Langevin et al., 2017]. Where the thickness \(H\) changes from one cell to the next, the transmissivity is then too low by about \(\Delta H / (2H)\). The grid MODFLOW 6 reads therefore has a computational top \(z'_\mathrm{top} = z_\mathrm{top} + \max H_1 - H_1\), with \(H_1\) the thickness of the top layer under the ground: every cell of the top layer gets the same thickness. A thickness that is the same everywhere gives \(z_\mathrm{top}\) back, so no file changes then. This is a numerical device of HydroModPy, not a USGS practice. Every package elevation (drains, EVT, streambeds, lake banks, barriers, the ocean), the initial heads and the run store keep the ground. Water above the ground stays in the aquifer up to \(z'_\mathrm{top}\), with its specific yield, where a cell topped at the ground would turn confined; the drains at the ground keep that rise to a few centimetres. Two limits. A lake column keeps its ground, since a raised top would reach into the lake, so the thickness still changes at the banks. Only the top of the top layer moves: a lower layer whose saturated part changes from cell to cell keeps the bias. Anyone who opens the native MODFLOW 6 files (flopy, ModelMuse) sees a top above the ground wherever the thickness varies. That is intended; the ground is topography in the run store. In the code: disv_grid_kwargs.

Flow between cells, and XT3D#

NPF receives \(K\), \(K_z = K/\beta\) and the convertible flag. With the two-point conductance (18), the flux between two cells is exact only when the line between the centres is perpendicular to the shared face and the conductivity is isotropic. XT3D adds the missing terms [Provost et al., 2017]. HydroModPy decides about it once per run; the rule and its trigger are on XT3D on irregular meshes. XT3D makes the matrix non-symmetric, so the IMS preset is promoted when needed (The solution: Newton and IMS). In the code: resolve_xt3d_decision.

Evaporation, drains, lakes and streams#

The laws, conductances and worked cells of these exits are on How water leaves the aquifer.

  • Evaporation. Under MODFLOW 6, EVTA receives a routed negative recharge (flow.sinks_sources.recharge.negative_to_evt) or, in soil mode, the ETP the soil could not meet. The loaded ETP is ignored outside soil mode, with a warning (Drains and evapotranspiration at the top of the model).

  • Drains. One drain in the first layer of every active cell that is not an ocean or stream constant-head cell. It sits at the ground surface or half a band depth below it. solver.sink_fill sets it to zero in closed depressions (Drains and evapotranspiration at the top of the model). In the code: build_drain_stress_period_data.

  • Lakes, streams and barriers. LAK, SFR, MVR and HFB exist under MODFLOW 6 only. LAK and SFR are built after the drains, so that the drains that coincide with a stream reach can be removed and the drainage of the hillslope can be handed to the stream or lake it flows to. MVR is built last, because it names the packages it moves water between (Lakes and reservoirs, Streams and the water mover).

Imposed heads and wells#

CHD imposes the head of the ocean, stream and lateral boundaries on their cells. MODFLOW 6 takes one CHD package, so the three supports are merged, and a cell claimed twice keeps the head written last. WEL adds the rate of each well. Under the Newton formulation the code sets auto_flow_reduce on WEL, so that a well that dewaters its cell tapers its extraction instead of making the head oscillate across the threshold. MODFLOW 6 reads it as a fraction of the cell height, and the option is one value per package: it is the smallest fraction that keeps the interval within a tenth of the aquifer below the ground in every well cell, 0.1 when the top is the ground. In the code: assemble_constant_head_stress_period_data, well_flow_reduce_fraction.

The solution: Newton and IMS#

The code writes NEWTON, and adds UNDER_RELAXATION when modflow6.runtime.mf6_newton_under_relaxation is true. Newton is on by default, because every layer is convertible. The UNDER_RELAXATION of the Newton option damps the head of a cell that Newton would push below its bottom. It is not the under-relaxation of IMS, which adjusts the update factor of the outer iteration.

The code passes the closure criteria, the iteration limits and the optional modflow6.runtime.mf6_linear_acceleration, modflow6.runtime.mf6_under_relaxation and modflow6.runtime.mf6_inner_rclose to the IMS package. The complexity, modflow6.runtime.mf6_ims_complexity, defaults to COMPLEX. A configured SIMPLE complexity is promoted to COMPLEX when XT3D is on, and to at least MODERATE under Newton, because SIMPLE assumes a symmetric, well-conditioned matrix. Two combinations are refused:

  • modflow6.runtime.mf6_newton and modflow6.runtime.mf6_enable_rewet together stop the run with [HMPY.E405] when the NPF package is built, because MODFLOW 6 forbids rewetting under the Newton formulation.

  • Conjugate-gradient acceleration under Newton stops with [HMPY.E406], because the Newton Jacobian is not symmetric and CG cannot solve it. The check runs before any grid is built. Use BICGSTAB, or leave modflow6.runtime.mf6_linear_acceleration unset.

In the code: resolve_ims_complexity.

The static ocean and side boundaries are not CHD rows. They are cells with a negative ibound in BAS, which imposes the starting head on them. Four things differ from MODFLOW 6.

  • The grid must be structured. NWT stops with MODFLOW NWT requires a structured grid. A [mesh_catchment] section does not change that: the solver keeps the grid it builds from the DEM.

  • The unconfined law is the upstream-weighted UPW package [Niswonger et al., 2011], with a smoothing of the saturated thickness set by modflownwt.runtime.nwt.thickfact.

  • EVT uses the topography. The code sets the EVT surface to the top lowered by flow.sinks_sources.etp.surface_offset and the extinction depth to flow.sinks_sources.etp.extinction_depth, with nevtop from modflownwt.runtime.evt.nevtop.   • No soil reservoir, lakes, streams or barriers. The checks listed under Limits stop the file.

In the code: assemble_flopy_packages.

Wetting and drying#

A cell dries when the head falls to its bottom. Under Newton, MODFLOW 6 smooths the saturated thickness near the bottom with a fraction of the thickness that it sets internally, and HydroModPy has no key for it [Langevin et al., 2017]. MODFLOW-NWT does the same in its SAT_THICK function, with the fraction read from thickfact [Niswonger et al., 2011]. The smoothing keeps the Newton step defined. It does not move the water table. The solvers differ in what they do next.

The vertical conductivity is a ratio on both, \(K_z = K / \beta\). The key is modflownwt.process_specific.vka for MODFLOW-NWT, with layvka set so that vka is read as a ratio, and modflow6.process_specific.vka for MODFLOW 6.

Sources and sinks. Recharge, wells, drains and constant heads are the same quantities in both. Two points differ.

  • The loaded ETP is a sink in MODFLOW-NWT, with its surface at the top minus flow.sinks_sources.etp.surface_offset and the extinction depth flow.sinks_sources.etp.extinction_depth. MODFLOW 6 draws none outside soil mode, and says so. In soil mode it writes the ETP the soil left unmet (The daily soil reservoir).

  • A negative recharge sent to EVT by flow.sinks_sources.recharge.negative_to_evt follows the same rule in both (Rain and evaporation: the forcing), with a different geometry: the model top and modflow6.process_specific.evt_extinction_depth in MODFLOW 6, the two etp keys above or fixed constants in MODFLOW-NWT.

Comparing the two. To compare the solvers, set the head test to the same value on both sides (modflownwt.runtime.nwt.headtol against the two dvclose keys): the residual tests do not measure the same thing. Validation lists the cases that run both solvers against the same closed-form solution, and runs both on the catchment of example 04, where their heads differ by less than a millimetre. Where the cell thickness changes from one cell to the next, the two conductances differ, and the computational top brings MODFLOW 6 back to MODFLOW-NWT: on the circular-island cases, whose top follows the ground over a flat bottom, both sit at the same distance from the closed form. Validation gives the thresholds and the reason for each. A threshold is a bound a test accepts, not a measured error, so it ranks nothing.

The Boussinesq solver#

The in-house solver writes one residual per triangle of a Gmsh mesh. It solves each stress period in simulation.time.substeps_per_period backward Euler steps. flow.runtime_backend picks the nonlinear solver, auto by default: the dense local engine up to 400 cells and the sparse scipy_sparse above; a dense engine asked by name stops on a larger mesh. flow.surface_interaction_model picks the closure for the water that reaches the surface. It builds recharge, wells, the side, stream and ocean imposed heads, and top drainage, and refuses the rest. Its flux between two cells uses the two cell centres only: on triangles far from equilateral it is not consistent, and the patch test of Validation shows the error that refinement does not remove. Its equations and closures are on Boussinesq theory.

Transport coupling#

A transport process attaches to the flow process that came before it. The planner binds each transport run to an earlier flow run of the right solver, in the order written in the file, and stops with requires an earlier process using flow/<solver> when there is none. The pairs are declared by each adapter as its requires attribute.

MODFLOW 6 GWT#

Modflow6Transport adds a GWT model to the MODFLOW 6 simulation of the flow run [Langevin et al., 2022]. It reuses the DISV grid and the idomain of the flow model, couples the two models with a GWF6-GWT6 exchange, and gives GWT its own IMS with BICGSTAB, because advection makes the matrix non-symmetric. The transport solve is linear, so it uses the configured complexity and no Newton promotion. What HydroModPy sets in the GWT model:

  • advection with the scheme of transport.modflow6gwt.parameters.scheme;

  • dispersion only when transport.modflow6gwt.parameters.disp_long or transport.modflow6gwt.parameters.diffu_coeff is above zero;

  • the mobile storage with transport.modflow6gwt.parameters.porosity. Without it, the code falls back on the specific yield and warns that this underestimates the pore volume, because the pore velocity is the Darcy flux divided by the porosity;

  • decay of order zero or one, from transport.modflow6gwt.parameters.react_order and transport.modflow6gwt.parameters.rate_decay.

Recharge is the only source of solute. Its concentration travels as the CONCENTRATION auxiliary variable of RCHA, and the source and sink mixing package reads it. Constant-head cells and wells bring water in at concentration zero.

MODFLOW 6 particle tracking#

modflow6_prt builds a PRT model on the same flow model. It needs the cell-by-cell velocity that modflow6.runtime.save_specific_discharge writes. Its keys are in [transport].

MODFLOW-NWT: MODPATH and MT3DMS#

MODPATH builds a MODPATH 6 input from the NWT run and stops if the flow model is not MODFLOW-NWT. MT3DMS needs the link file that the LMT package writes. NWT adds LMT only when an MT3DMS process is planned after it. The file name is modflownwt.runtime.lmt.output_file_name. In the code: _requires_mt3dms_link.

Assumptions#

  • Flow is saturated, in one phase, and the head is the only unknown. Water above the water table is not modelled: the code writes no UZF package, and the soil reservoir of The daily soil reservoir is a separate daily model that hands a recharge to the solver.

  • Every layer is convertible: it can be confined or unconfined from one step to the next.

  • The horizontal conductivity is isotropic. The vertical one is the horizontal one divided by one ratio for the whole model, or a per-cell \(K_z\) field.

Limits#

The capability matrix is generated on Solvers. The refusals, with their messages, are these.

  • Grid. MODFLOW-NWT works on the structured grid only. The Boussinesq solver works on Gmsh triangles only, and a dense runtime asked by name on a mesh of more than 400 cells.

  • Boundaries. Lakes, reservoirs, SFR and barriers need MODFLOW 6. The stream boundary needs MODFLOW 6 or Boussinesq. When solver.backend names modflow_nwt, the file is refused when it is read if it asks for flow.active_bc with sfr, lake, reservoir or stream, for flow barriers or a dam cutoff wall, or for a head initial condition of type spinup_cyclic. does not support the 'sfr' boundary is the start of the message for the first case, and does not support flow barriers / dam cutoff walls (HFB); they require the 'modflow6' backend. is the whole message for barriers. An unstructured mesh stops the build with MODFLOW NWT requires a structured grid.

  • Soil reservoir. [flow.sinks_sources.recharge.soil] is wired to MODFLOW 6 only stops a file that asks for it with another solver.

  • ETP. MODFLOW 6 draws no ETP outside soil mode, and only logs a warning. Boussinesq rejects it.

  • Hotstart and cyclic spin-up. flow.restart_from and a cyclic spin-up need MODFLOW 6, the only solver that declares the two capabilities.

  • Newton. Newton with rewet (HMPY.E405) and Newton with conjugate gradients (HMPY.E406) are refused (The solution: Newton and IMS).

  • Not represented. The code writes no unsaturated-zone package (UZF), no variable-density package (BUY), no subsidence package (CSUB), and no GHB or RIV package.

The default of 1 integrates a whole period in a single backward-Euler step, which is not a neutral choice: on a seasonally recharged hillslope drained by DRN cells it moves the monthly discharge by 4 to 28 % against a refined run, across the whole plausible bedrock range, and a calibration scored on discharge absorbs that into the fitted parameters. Each period starts at the step simulation.time.substeps_per_period asked for and MF6 subdivides below it only where the solver cannot converge (e.g. littoral wet/dry under Newton); it never grows past that step, so turning ATS on cannot coarsen a run. # mf6_newton = trueEnable the MODFLOW 6 Newton-Raphson formulation. expert
Path
modflow6.runtime.mf6_newton
More

Catchment cells are always convertible (unconfined), so Newton with under-relaxation is the robust default. Unlike MODFLOW-NWT, it builds the horizontal conductance from the full cell thickness times the upstream saturation. The grid MODFLOW 6 reads therefore has a computational top that gives every cell of the top layer the same thickness, so that this conductance is the one of NWT; the ground stays in every package and in the run store.

More

The solver reports the figure on every run; unset, it is recorded and nothing acts on it, so a run at twelve per cent is ranked beside one that closed even though part of the water it routed came from nowhere. There is no default because there is no universal value: a steady solve on a coarse mesh closes to a fraction of a per cent, a transient one with a lake and a routed network legitimately sits higher.
