The daily soil reservoir#

Each day, a soil store takes the rain, loses water to evapotranspiration, and sends the water it cannot hold down to the water table. This page gives the equations the code solves, what they assume, and where they stop being true. It does not say how to set up a run: for that, see step 6 of the staircase.

Note

Scope. The reservoir runs under MODFLOW 6 only. A file that declares flow.sinks_sources.recharge.soil with another flow solver is refused when it is read. The equations below are the ones of hydromodpy/physics/hydrology/soil_reservoir/kernel.py.

The daily balance#

The code applies the steps below in this order, for every column and every day.

1. Curve-number runoff (optional)#

Read only when curve_number is set, between 30 and 98. Without it, \(Q_t = 0\) and the step costs nothing: the result is the same, bit for bit, as without the term.

The runoff is the SCS curve-number runoff as the HELP model uses it [Schroeder and others, 1994]. The conversion of a class II number to the dry (class I) and wet (class III) numbers is the one of Chow et al. [1988], equations 5.5.6 and 5.5.7. The code makes these choices:

  • The number in the file is the class II one. The code derives the other two.

  • The wetness of the soil is the store at the end of the day before, as a share of its capacity (next equation).

  • The retention \(\sigma_t\), in mm, moves linearly with \(w_t\) between its value at the dry number (\(w_t = 0\)) and its value at the wet number (\(w_t = 1\)) [Schroeder and others, 1994].

  • The initial abstraction is \(0.2\,\sigma_t\), with no other choice.

  • The rain that infiltrates is \(P_t - Q_t\). The runoff \(Q_t\) is taken before the soil sees the rain, so the soil gets less of it.

(6)#\[ w_t = \min\!\left(1,\ \max\!\left(0,\ \frac{S_{t-1}}{\mathrm{RU}}\right)\right), \qquad w_t = 1 \text{ if } \mathrm{RU} = 0 . \]

2. Bypass#

A constant share \(b\) of the infiltrating rain skips the soil. The rest is added to the store:

(7)#\[ B_t = b\,(P_t - Q_t), \qquad W_t = S_{t-1} + (1 - b)\,(P_t - Q_t). \]

Here \(b\) is bypass_fraction. \(W_t\) is the water in the soil after the rain and before any evaporation.

3. Evapotranspiration#

The soil evaporates at the full rate \(k_c E_t\) while it holds more than \(\alpha\,\mathrm{RU}\). Below that, the rate falls linearly with the store. It can never exceed what the soil holds:

(8)#\[ \psi_t = \min\!\left(1,\ \frac{W_t}{\alpha\,\mathrm{RU}}\right), \qquad \mathrm{ETR}_t = \min\bigl(W_t,\ k_c\,E_t\,\psi_t\bigr), \]

with \(\psi_t = 1\) if \(\mathrm{RU} = 0\). The two symbols are alpha and kc, and ru is the capacity. \(\alpha\) is the \(1 - p\) of FAO-56 [Allen et al., 1998] and the \(LP\) of HBV [Seibert and Vis, 2012].

4. Overflow#

What exceeds the capacity leaves the soil. Nothing else does:

(9)#\[ X_t = \max\bigl(0,\ W_t - \mathrm{ETR}_t - \mathrm{RU}\bigr), \qquad S_t = W_t - \mathrm{ETR}_t - X_t . \]

5. Transit through the unsaturated zone#

The overflow and the bypass enter one linear reservoir of time constant \(\tau\), transit_time. The inflow \(I_t = X_t + B_t\) is taken as constant over the day, and the reservoir is integrated exactly over that day:

(10)#\[ R_t = \tau I_t + (R_{t-1} - \tau I_t)\,e^{-1/\tau}, \qquad O_t = I_t - (R_t - R_{t-1}). \]

\(O_t\) is the water that reaches the water table. If \(\tau = 0\), then \(O_t = I_t\) and \(R_t = R_{t-1}\).

6. Routing of the curve-number runoff#

The runoff \(Q_t\) goes through the same exact reservoir, with the time constant \(\tau_q\), runoff_time, and its own store \(C_t\). The routed runoff is called \(Q^r_t\). If \(\tau_q = 0\), then \(Q^r_t = Q_t\).

7. Delay of the curve-number runoff#

A pure delay of \(L\) days, runoff_lag, follows. Write \(L = n + \varphi\) with \(n\) a whole number of days and \(0 \le \varphi < 1\). The delayed runoff is a linear interpolation between two whole-day shifts:

(11)#\[ Q^L_t = (1 - \varphi)\,Q^r_{t-n} + \varphi\,Q^r_{t-n-1} . \]

A day that falls before day 1 of the run reads zero, or, with spinup = "first_year", the days at the end of the last repeated first year, which stand for the days before the run. The delay is not a store: it moves water in time and keeps its volume, except the part pushed beyond the last day of the run.

8. Fluxes per unit of column area#

The stores above are per unit of unsealed area. The sealed share \(f\), impervious_fraction, gets no infiltration and no evaporation. The fluxes of the column are:

(12)#\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} N_t &= (1 - f)\,O_t,\\ \text{ETR}^{\text{col}}_t &= (1 - f)\,\mathrm{ETR}_t,\\ \text{residual ETP}_t &= (1 - f)\,\bigl(k_c\,E_t - \mathrm{ETR}_t\bigr),\\ \text{runoff}_t &= f\,P_t + (1 - f)\,Q^L_t . \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

\(N_t\) is the recharge MODFLOW 6 receives. The residual ETP is the demand \(k_c E_t\) that the soil could not meet.

The balance closes#

Over any run, per column and per day:

(13)#\[ P_t = \mathrm{ETR}^{\text{col}}_t + N_t + \text{runoff}_t + \Delta\bigl[(1 - f)(S + R + C)\bigr]_t + \Delta\Lambda_t , \]

where \(\Lambda_t\) is the running total of runoff held back by the delay (runoff_lag). The function balance_residual computes the difference between the two sides. On 400 days of random rain and ETP over three columns, with a sealed share, a bypass, a transit time, a curve number and a delay all switched on, the largest residual is under \(10^{-13}\) mm: rounding error only.

The starting state#

With spinup = "none", the soil starts full and the other stores empty: \(S_0 = \mathrm{RU}\), \(R_0 = C_0 = 0\).

With spinup = "first_year", the default, the first 365 days of rain and ETP are run again and again, starting from that full soil. The loop stops when no store of any column moves by \(0.1\) mm or more over one repetition, or after 20 repetitions. The state at the end of the last repetition is \(S_0\), \(R_0\) and \(C_0\). If 20 repetitions are not enough, the run stops with an error. A run shorter than 365 days is refused with this option.

Columns and capacity classes#

Without data.soil_capacity, each cell of the rain and ETP grid is one column and the same RU applies to all. With it, the pixels of the raster in each cell are sorted by capacity and cut into ten classes of equal pixel count. Each class is a column, with a capacity \(\mathrm{RU} \times (\text{class mean} / \text{raster mean})\). The value given in the file is therefore the mean capacity, and the raster only spreads it. The basin value of any flux gives every cell the same weight, and every class of a cell its share of the pixels.

How much recharge, by capacity#

\(\mathrm{RU}\) is the parameter the recharge depends on most. A larger store keeps more of the winter rain for the summer, when the plants take it back, so the recharge falls as the capacity grows.

Assumptions#

  • The rain of a day reaches the soil before it evaporates, the same day. The evaporation stress of day \(t\) is computed on the store after the rain.

  • The rain and the ETP are constant over the day.

  • All the water above RU leaves the soil as drainage. There is no saturation-excess runoff term in the kernel.

  • The bypass is a constant share of the infiltrating rain, the same in every season.

  • The unsaturated zone is one linear reservoir per column, with one time constant for the whole catchment.

  • The sealed share receives rain and nothing else: it neither infiltrates nor evaporates, and its runoff is immediate.

  • The curve-number runoff reads the wetness of the day before, not the one after the rain.

  • Columns do not exchange water with each other.

  • One value of \(\alpha\), \(k_c\), \(f\), \(b\), \(\tau\), CN, \(\tau_q\) and \(L\) applies to the whole catchment and to the whole run. Only RU varies, through the capacity classes.

  • The rain is one total grid: snow counts as rain on the day it falls. The file is refused unless components = ["total"].

Limits#

  • No feedback from the aquifer. The soil store never reads the water table. A shallow water table does not reduce the capacity. The only link back is the residual ETP, which MODFLOW 6 may draw from the water table (Where the results go).

  • Daily forcing only. The rain and the ETP must have one value per day and per cell, with no gap and no negative value, on a standard calendar, with the rain and the ETP on the same grid, in the CRS of the model. Otherwise the run stops before the reservoir is computed.

  • No groundwater memory. The transit time is limited to 30 days, so that the slow response comes from MODFLOW 6 and not from this reservoir.

  • Curve number and delay. \(\tau_q\) and \(L\) are read only when curve_number is set. A fractional delay spreads a one-day pulse over two days. The part of the delayed runoff that falls after the last day of the run is not represented.

  • First period. A steady first stress period is refused, since it would hold the recharge of one day only.

  • SIM2 drainage is not a summer reference across 2016. Over the catchment of example 04, the SIM2 drainage (DRAINC) never falls below about 0.2 mm/day from 2016 on: its 30-day minimum is 0.29, 0.23 and 0.30 mm/day in 2016, 2017 and 2018, against 0.00 to 0.04 mm/day in 2012 to 2015, on all nine cells of the grid alike. A recharge from this reservoir falls to zero in a dry summer; compare it with the SIM2 drainage period by period, never across that step.

  • No other runoff. Water that leaves saturated ground is not a term of this model. In a run, it leaves through the drains of MODFLOW 6.

Where the results go#

The reservoir hands three daily grids to the rest of the run, in mm/day on the cells of the rain grid.

  • The recharge goes to the MODFLOW 6 recharge package. A file that also loads a recharge, through data.types, is refused.

  • The residual ETP goes to the MODFLOW 6 evapotranspiration package, which draws it from the water table. The rate falls linearly to zero at flow.sinks_sources.etp.extinction_depth below the drain elevation of the cell. This needs "etp" in flow.active_sinks_sources.

  • The runoff goes where a loaded runoff would go: the stream networks, the lakes, and the discharge that a calibration compares with the gauge. A file that also loads a runoff, through data.types, is refused, because the same water would be counted twice.
