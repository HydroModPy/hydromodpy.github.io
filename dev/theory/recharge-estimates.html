Recharge estimated from the data#

A [recharge_estimates] section asks for a range of recharge on the catchment, computed from the loaded weather, gauge and piezometers before the solver runs. None of these numbers enters the model. They bound the answer the way the ESPERE tool of the BRGM does (report RP-69538-FR, Lanini et al. 2016), and the run’s own recharge is set beside them, so one look says whether the run sits inside what the data allow.

The weather is reduced to one basin series, the mean of the grid cells or of the stations. The gauge is turned into mm/day over the catchment area. A gap of 3 days or less in the gauge, 5 days or less in a piezometer, is filled by a straight line, and a longer gap cuts the series. A year counts when its series covers 90 % of its days. A method whose data is missing is skipped with a sentence that says why.

The formulas#

Turc gives an effective rainfall \(P_e = P\,(1 - 1/\sqrt{0.9 + (P/L)^2})\) with \(L = 300 + 25T + 0.05T^3\). The cube is the one of the method and of ESPERE 2.44, and the manual of ESPERE prints a square by mistake. It is a rainfall that has not yet infiltrated, and the table says so.

Guttman and Zuckerman give three straight lines of the yearly rain, \(0.45(P - 180)\) under 600 mm, \(0.88(P - 410)\) under 1000 mm, and \(0.97(P - 463)\) from 1000 mm. ESPERE leaves a year of exactly 1000 mm on no line. Under 180 mm the recharge is zero.

Kessler reads the share of the yearly rain that falls from January to April, corrected by how wet the previous autumn was against the mean autumn, and turns it into a rate of infiltration by the cubic of his abacus. The correction goes by steps of \(|CPR|\) up to 0.70, then 15. ESPERE leaves a gap between 0.70 and 0.71. The first year has no value.

The three filters split the daily discharge into a quick part and a baseflow, read as the recharge that leaves the aquifer. Wallingford joins the smoothed minima of blocks of 5 days. Chapman and Maxwell use one recession constant \(k\), Eckhardt adds the largest share of baseflow \(\mathrm{BFI}_{max}\),

(21)#\[Q_b(t) = \min\!\left(Q(t),\ \frac{(1 - \mathrm{BFI}_{max})\,k\,Q_b(t-1) + (1 - k)\,\mathrm{BFI}_{max}\,Q(t)}{1 - k\,\mathrm{BFI}_{max}}\right).\]

Unset, \(k\) is read on the falling limbs of the gauge of 5 days or more, their first 2 days dropped, and \(\mathrm{BFI}_{max}\) is the largest yearly index of Wallingford, as ESPERE does.

The water table fluctuation multiplies the rises of the water table by the specific yield. With correction = "recession" it adds to each rise the decline the rain interrupted: the daily declines of the recession before it are fitted as an exponential of time and extended to the start of the next one. ESPERE fits the logarithm of the raw elevation, so its answer moves with the datum. Fitting the declines does not. The estimate is proportional to \(S_y\), which the section asks for.

Wallingford also gives the infiltration ratio ESPERE v2 proposes for its soil budgets, the lowest yearly baseflow index of the record. The table writes it as a row infiltration_ratio, and the user copies it into infiltration_ratio when the soil should send the rest of its excess to the streams.

The same Wallingford filter also judges a run. The run metric bfi_gap is the baseflow index of the simulated discharge minus the one of the gauge, both filtered on the same days of the metrics window, and the calibration criterion bfi_error is its absolute value. It is the index of the whole window, not the lowest yearly index above. It asks whether the simulated stream is fed by its aquifer in the share the gauge shows: a slower aquifer, or drains that rarely overflow, raise it. It is blind to the volume and to the timing, since a simulated series twice the observed one scores zero, so it goes beside a fit such as nse, never alone. The filter reads blocks of five days, so it needs a daily discharge: a monthly run leaves bfi_gap empty, and the criterion is refused outside a transient run with a daily step.

The closing factor is the number that would scale the run’s recharge and runoff onto the gauged volume over their common days. Abherve (2022) multiplies the SURFEX recharge by it before he calibrates on that same gauge. Here it is only printed, so a user who wants it copies it into factor knowing what it does.

The storage loop#

With a soil reservoir and a gauge, the section also writes, month by month, the rain minus the soil’s ETR beside the gauged discharge. The figure water_balance_loop draws them: above the 1:1 line the catchment stores water, below it the aquifer drains, and the shape of the loop reads the size of the store (Abherve 2022, section 2.2.3, after Schmidt et al. 2020).

The soil laws and a loaded product, month by month#

recharge_estimates.soil_methods runs each soil law of the soil reservoir on the same rain, ETP, ru, crop coefficient, snow and sealed share as the run. Only the storage law changes. A run without a soil section of its own gives the laws recharge_estimates.soil, with its ru written, and never reads it. In a run whose soil computes the recharge, recharge_estimates.compare_loaded loads data.recharge and data.runoff, SIM2 DRAINC and RUNC for instance, and sets them aside before the model is bound, so the model never receives them. Each law and the product give yearly recharge rows, which join the range above, and monthly rows of recharge and runoff beside the run’s own and beside the rain and the ETP. The figure recharge_signals draws them month by month and recharge_regime draws their mean year. One ru is not one soil, because the ru that closes the budget differs from law to law: on the Nancon over 2015-2020 it is 187 mm with fao56 and 376 mm with dingman.

These figures need no solve. hmp run <file> --until prepare_solver writes the run and its tables in a minute or two, then hmp show <run> --figure recharge_signals --to signals.png draws one.

A steady run on a yearly formula#

Turc, Guttman and Zuckerman and Kessler give one value a year, so they never force a transient run. A steady run may take one as its rate with data.recharge.formula. The data step runs the formula on each complete calendar year of the run, on the basin mean of the rain (and of the temperature for Turc), and lays the mean over the years on the grid of the rain. Turc gives an effective rainfall, which the run takes whole, so the drains do the share a RIPE would do. A transient run, a soil section and a scenario beside the formula are refused.

Where the results go#

The run prints one block at the end of the data step, one line per method with its mean, its range and its years. It writes the table recharge_estimates, one row per method, year and quantity, and the figures recharge_estimates and water_balance_loop read it.

# method = "fao56"Storage law of the soil, the one thing that differs between methods. experimental
Path
recharge_estimates.soil.method
More

"fao56" lowers ETR linearly below alpha x ru (Allen et al. 1998). "thornthwaite" meets the full demand while water is left (Thornthwaite 1948). "dingman" empties the soil as an exponential of the deficit on dry days (Dingman 2008). "edijatno_michel" is the production store of GR4J without its percolation (Edijatno and Michel 1989). The sealed share, the curve number, the bypass, the transit and the snow work the same around every law.

# spinup = "first_year"State of the soil on the first day. experimental
Path
recharge_estimates.soil.spinup
More

"first_year" repeats the first 365 days of the run until the storage at the end of that year moves by less than 0.1 mm, 20 times at most; the run must last 364 days (52 weeks) at least, and a 364-day run repeats those 364 days. "none" starts from a full soil, the state of a soil in a wet winter. Beside a cyclic spin-up of the aquifer (flow.ic.kind = "spinup_cyclic"), the two starts are computed over two windows: the soil settles on the first year, the heads on the whole run. That pair is allowed without a warning, because it is sometimes what the study wants.

Values
  • "first_year" : Repeat the first year until the soil storage settles.
  • "none" : Start from a full soil and an empty transit store.
Accepted
