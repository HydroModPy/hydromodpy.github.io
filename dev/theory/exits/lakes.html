Lakes and reservoirs#

A lake is a boundary package laid on the aquifer grid. Its cells are made inactive in their top layers, and the LAK package of MODFLOW 6 holds the storage and the exchange through connections it builds itself. The stage is a result. The keys of a lake are in [flow.sinks_sources.lakes].

The balance#

The LAK package of MODFLOW 6 solves a water balance of the lake: the stage \(s_L\) is the unknown, and the volume \(V_L\) and the surface area \(A_L\) are looked up in the stage-volume-area table of the lake. The lake gains rain, runoff and managed inflow, loses evaporation, withdrawal and outlet discharge, and exchanges with the aquifer through its connections (input guide, [Langevin et al., 2017], chapter 7). The outlets go to the outside, to another lake or to the mover (Streams and the water mover).

HydroModPy checks the table before MODFLOW 6 sees it. The table has at least two rows. The stage increases strictly, the volume increases strictly with it, the surface area does not decrease, and no volume or area is negative.

In the code: build_lake_table.

The connections#

MODFLOW 6 gives each connection the conductance of the bed and the aquifer in series, \(C^\mathrm{lak}_c\), from the leakance \(\lambda_b\) and the contact area. A leakance of zero seals the bed. HydroModPy builds two kinds of connection for each lake column:

  • one vertical connection to the first active cell under the occupied layers: the leakage towards the aquifer, through the vertical conductivity;

  • one horizontal connection per occupied layer across every edge shared with an active neighbour that is not a lake cell: the bank seepage, through the horizontal conductivity. Its width is the length of the shared edge, its length the distance from the neighbour centre to the edge, and its elevations those of the neighbour layer clipped to the vertical extent of the lake.

A cutoff wall seals the horizontal connections to the cells behind it.

In the code: build_lake_connectiondata.

Two ways to hold the footprint#

In the fixed-area mode, the footprint cells are inactive and the table alone gives the storage. In the active-shore mode, dynamic_area, the bed cells stay active with the carved bathymetry as their top. A cell exchanges with the lake while it is under water, and recharges as land when the shore falls below its bed. The drain rows of those cells are removed: a drain there would send the leakage of the lake straight back to the streams, through the aquifer, the drain and the mover, and the exchange would recirculate.

What falls on the lake#

The rain and the evaporation of a lake are the catchment means of the loaded precipitation and ETP, unless the lake declares its own. The runoff is the catchment runoff rate times the catchment area \(A\). It reaches the lake directly only when no stream network routes the runoff; otherwise it arrives through the reaches and the mover (Rain and evaporation: the forcing). A declared forcing wins over these defaults.

The managed inflow and the withdrawal are set to zero in the steady periods. A steady period has no storage term, and a managed flux that does not balance the natural budget has no equilibrium stage: the solve would diverge.

By default the stage is free from the first period. With steady_stage_hold, and a run that starts with a steady period, the build holds it at the starting stage (LAK status constant) through the steady warm-up, and frees it on the first transient period. A managed reservoir whose observed level is far from the natural equilibrium then keeps that level, and the aquifer settles around it.

The package takes one smoothing depth for the wet and dry transition of the lake edge, surfdep: the largest declared among the lakes.

The mask on the other packages#

The lake cells carry no recharge and no aquifer evapotranspiration: the lake takes its own rain and evaporates through LAK. Without the mask, MODFLOW 6 would hand the recharge of an inactive lake cell to the first active cell below it, and the rain would be counted twice (Drains and evapotranspiration at the top of the model).

Limits#

  • A lake has one stage. The volume and the surface area follow the table, and the lake exchanges with the cells under and beside its footprint, and with no other.

  • A lake needs MODFLOW 6. The check stops a file that declares one with another solver.

  • A lake in soil mode needs a stream network. The check refuses a lake without "sfr" when the soil reservoir is declared, because the water that seeps out of the ground reaches the lake only through the streams.

  • The active-shore mode is harder for the solver. Many cells change between wet and dry at once. A larger surfdep helps, and blurs the shoreline.
