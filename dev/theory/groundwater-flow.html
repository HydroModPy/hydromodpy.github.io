The groundwater flow problem#

What head does the aquifer hold, in every cell and at every time? This page gives the geometry HydroModPy builds for that head, the properties that enter it and the state it starts from. The flow equation itself is MODFLOW’s and is read at its source. For the same subject in plain words, see What the model computes. What each solver supports is listed in Solvers.

Note

Scope. Three solvers read the same [flow] section: MODFLOW 6 (the default), MODFLOW-NWT and the in-house Boussinesq solver. They solve the same physical problem and not the same discrete equations. The balance of the two MODFLOW solvers is in What the solvers solve. The Boussinesq form is on Boussinesq theory. What enters and leaves the aquifer (recharge, drains, streams, lakes, wells) has its own pages: Rain and evaporation: the forcing, The daily soil reservoir and How water leaves the aquifer. Where the one-layer reading stops being true is at the end of Limits.

The domain#

Two surfaces bound the aquifer.

The top \(z_\mathrm{top}\) is the digital elevation model of the catchment, read from data.dem. By default the grid takes the value of the DEM at the centre of each cell, and nothing else moves the top. modflow6.sgrid.top_sampling.mode can ask for a statistic over the pixels of the cell instead, with a lower value for the cells that hold a channel (5. The top of a cell).

The bottom \(z_\mathrm{bot}\) comes from a depth model, chosen by domain.depth_model.kind. Four kinds exist:

A flat substratum is an elevation, not a depth: where the land surface drops under it, no aquifer is left. A raster kind:

  1. reprojects the raster onto the grid of the top;

  2. refuses the run if one cell of the domain has no raster value, so the raster must cover the whole extent of the domain, buffer included when geographic.domain_extent is "box";

  3. applies \(\lambda\) and \(\zeta\);

  4. lowers the bottom to \(z_\mathrm{top} - H_\mathrm{min}\) wherever it would come closer to the top than that, and logs a warning that counts the cells;

  5. refuses the run if every cell needs that correction, since no aquifer would remain.

Take a top of 120 m everywhere and a raster that holds 40 m, or 85 m for the elevation kind, in every cell but one. The table gives the bottom of that cell, as build_domain returns it (the check ran the function).

The solver grid and its layers#

The grid samples \(z_\mathrm{top}\) and \(z_\mathrm{bot}\) at the centre of each cell (the cell layout is on The mesh). A cell with no finite value in either surface becomes inactive in every layer. The grid is refused when \(z_\mathrm{bot} \ge z_\mathrm{top}\) on a valid cell. On a runtime mesh, a warning names the columns thicker than 500 m and than 10 times the median: a raster nodata value read as an elevation produces them (8. The checks the code applies).

The thickness is then cut into \(n_\mathrm{lay}\) layers. The bottom of layer \(j\) is

(14)#\[ z_{\mathrm{bot},j} = z_\mathrm{top} - H\,p_j , \qquad p_{n_\mathrm{lay}} = 1 . \]

The shares \(p_j\) are the same for every cell. Three rules build them, chosen by modflow6.sgrid.vertical.genmtd_lay:

genmtd_lay

\(p_j\)

constant

\(j / n_\mathrm{lay}\)

decay, with \(\rho\) read from modflow6.sgrid.vertical.lay_decay

\((1 - \rho^{j}) / (1 - \rho^{n_\mathrm{lay}})\)

list

the running sum of modflow6.sgrid.vertical.lay_proportions

The decay rule needs \(\rho > 1\), and the file is refused otherwise: the layers then thicken with depth. With \(\rho = 2\) and four layers they hold 1, 2, 4 and 8 fifteenths of the column, from the top down. A list must sum to 1. With one layer, \(p_1 = 1\) and the column is one cell. MODFLOW-NWT reads the same keys under modflownwt.sgrid.vertical.

What the layers can represent. Every layer reuses the planar cells and their connections, and only the top and the bottom of the column vary from cell to cell. Geology therefore varies through the properties of each layer and cell (The properties), not through surfaces of its own. A stratigraphy whose interfaces cross the proportions, a layer that disappears, or a connection that changes from one layer to the next is not represented. MODFLOW 6 builds every grid, structured or not, as a vertex grid (DISV), never as DISU. The Boussinesq solver has no layers: it works on one triangle per column. In the code: _compute_layer_proportions.

A depth factor. A property can carry a field_vertical_profile. The value of layer \(j\) in a column is the value of the field times \(f(d_j)\), where \(d_j\) is the depth of the middle of the layer below \(z_\mathrm{top}\). For mode = "exponential":

(15)#\[ f(d) = e^{-d/d_c}, \qquad \text{or } \max\bigl(e^{-d/d_c},\ f_\mathrm{min}\bigr) \text{ when } f_\mathrm{min} \text{ is set.} \]

Without min_factor, the factor has no floor and tends to zero with depth. For mode = "tabulated", the factor is a linear or stepwise interpolation of the table you give, equal to the first and last factor outside the table. With one layer, \(d_j\) is half the thickness: the profile then gives one factor per cell and no variation with depth.

Vertical conductivity. MODFLOW 6 reads \(K_z = K / \beta\), with \(\beta\) the modflow6.process_specific.vka, layer by layer, so a depth factor on \(K\) reaches \(K_z\). A parameter with the id Kv replaces it with a conductivity per cell, and a file that gives Kv and a vka other than 1 is refused.

The code refuses a \(K\) that is zero, negative or not finite on an active cell. A heterogeneous \(K\) whose zones do not cover the whole domain produces exactly that. In the code: FieldParam.vertical_factor.

What the solvers solve#

MODFLOW 6 and MODFLOW-NWT write Darcy’s law and mass conservation for the head in each cell, by control volumes, with one head per cell at its centre [Harbaugh, 2005, Langevin et al., 2017]; chapter 2 of Langevin et al. [2017] gives the control-volume form. The derivation is not repeated here. Three pieces of it are used by other pages of this site, so they are written once.

In a catchment the aquifer is mostly unconfined. The head can sit below the top of a cell, and then the thickness that carries flow is the saturated thickness

(16)#\[ B(h) = \min\bigl(\max(h - z_\mathrm{bot},\ 0),\ H\bigr) . \]

For a cell \(n\), the balance of one time step is

(17)#\[ \sum_{m \in \mathcal{N}(n)} G_{nm}\,(h_m - h_n) + \sum_{p} \Phi_{n,p} = \frac{1}{\delta t}\Bigl[\,S_s\,a_n\,B_n\,(h_n - h_n^\mathrm{old}) + S_y\,a_n\,(B_n - B_n^\mathrm{old})\Bigr] . \]

The left side is the water that enters the cell during the step: the exchange with the neighbours and the stress packages. The right side is the change of the volume stored. \(h^\mathrm{old}\) is the head at the start of the step. This is the one statement of the balance on the site; the solver pages refer to it. Between two cells of a layer, the conductance joins the two half cells in series:

(18)#\[ \frac{1}{G_{nm}} = \frac{c_{nm}}{K_n\,B_n\,w_{nm}} + \frac{c_{mn}}{K_m\,B_m\,w_{nm}} , \]

with \(w_{nm}\) the width of the shared face and \(c_{nm}\) the distance from the centre of \(n\) to that face. Under the Newton formulation, the series is built with the full cell thicknesses and multiplied by the saturated fraction of the upstream cell ([Langevin et al., 2017]).

What HydroModPy sets:

  • Every cell is convertible. icelltype = 1 in the MODFLOW 6 NPF package, iconvert = 1 in STO, laytyp = 1 in the MODFLOW-NWT UPW package. The specific yield \(S_y\) acts while the water table is inside the cell, and \(S_s\) above the top of a full cell. In example 04, \(S_y = 0.05\) and \(S_s B \le 3 \times 10^{-4}\), a ratio of about 170.

  • No CELL_AVERAGING option. The series average of the equation above is the MODFLOW 6 default.

  • Newton, with under-relaxation, by default. MODFLOW-NWT is the Newton formulation of MODFLOW-2005 [Niswonger et al., 2011], on the same convertible layers and a structured grid. The options and the stopping tests are on The solution: Newton and IMS.

  • Voronoi faces bisect the centres. That is the case the two-point form of (18) needs. Elsewhere, the XT3D correction is on XT3D on irregular meshes.

  • The Boussinesq solver writes the same balance on triangles. Its edge flux takes the harmonic mean of \(K\) and the arithmetic mean of \(B\) of the two cells, and its storage has no \(S_s\). See Boussinesq theory.

The smoothing of \(B\) near the bottom of a cell is on The solvers of the aquifer.

Time#

The run is cut into stress periods of length \(\Delta t\), one per forcing value (Rain and evaporation: the forcing). Within a period the forcing is constant. Each period is cut into \(n_\mathrm{sub}\) equal time steps \(\delta t = \Delta t / n_\mathrm{sub}\), and the time integration is the backward Euler scheme of [Langevin et al., 2017]: (17) is written with the stress and the conductances at the end of the step. The time step multiplier is 1, so the steps of a period are equal. The one exception is modflow6.runtime.mf6_ats: with it, MODFLOW 6 may cut a transient step further where the solver does not converge, never above the step asked for. The Boussinesq solver takes one step per period.

The default of 1 integrates a whole period in a single backward-Euler step, which is not a neutral choice: on a seasonally recharged hillslope drained by DRN cells it moves the monthly discharge by 4 to 28 % against a refined run, across the whole plausible bedrock range, and a calibration scored on discharge absorbs that into the fitted parameters. The solver time step is a numerical choice: a period solved in one step differs from the same period in several. Time steps gives the size of that difference on a test case.

flow.flow_regime picks the regime. A steady run is one steady period over the whole [simulation.time] window, whatever step the file writes. In a transient run, flow.first_period_steady marks the first period as steady, in the three solvers. In a steady period the right side of the balance is zero, the storage plays no part, and the heads settle where the forcing of that period puts them. Example 04 states the regime:

In every case, a cell on an imposed head (an ocean, a stream or a side boundary) starts at that head. top is a full aquifer; the card of flow.ic.h.type gives the default.

The equilibrium. For steady_state, the code first solves an auxiliary steady problem with the same solver, the same grid and the same properties. Its recharge is a constant, set by flow.ic.h.source: the mean of the whole record, or the rate that you state. The result is \(h_0\):

\[ \nabla\!\cdot\!\bigl(K\,B(h^\star)\,\nabla h^\star\bigr) + \bar N + q_\mathrm{bc}(h^\star) = 0 , \qquad h_0 = h^\star , \]

where \(\bar N\) is the constant recharge and \(q_\mathrm{bc}\) the exchanges with the boundaries per unit area, held at the value of the first period (flow.ic.h.boundary_condition_policy).

The settled cycle. For spinup_cyclic, the run repeats its own period. Cycle \(c + 1\) starts from the heads where cycle \(c\) ended, \(h_\mathrm{end}^{(c)}\), and the first cycle starts from flow.ic.h.first_cycle_from. The loop stops when

(19)#\[ \max_n \bigl|\,h^{(c+1)}_{\mathrm{end},n} - h^{(c)}_{\mathrm{end},n}\,\bigr| \le \varepsilon_h , \]

with \(\varepsilon_h\) the flow.ic.h.tol_head, or after flow.ic.h.max_cycles cycles. The maximum counts every cell, not the mean.

When the first period is steady. With flow.first_period_steady at its default, period 0 is solved as a steady state, and the heads above are only the first guess of that solve. They change the convergence and not the state reached. To make the run start from a state you choose, set it to false (see [flow.ic]).

A run can also restart from the last time step of an earlier run (flow.restart_from). That state replaces \(h_0\). In the code: build_head_initial_condition_array.

How water leaves the aquifer

Recharge and injection are positive when water enters the aquifer, and a well with a negative rate pumps. Where no boundary is activated, the edge of the domain carries no flow. The two keys that activate them are flow.active_bc and flow.active_sinks_sources.

Limits#

  • Shallow and unconfined. Every cell is convertible. The code is built for the saturated zone of a catchment, from the surface down to a substratum that you place. The thickness is chosen, not computed.

  • No unsaturated flow. The code builds no UZF package. The water that reaches the water table is a recharge, given or computed by the daily soil store of The daily soil reservoir.

  • No density-dependent flow, no compaction, no delayed yield. The code builds neither a BUY nor a CSUB package, and the specific yield releases its water at once.

  • Constant forcing, impervious edges. The forcing is constant over a stress period. The bottom and the sides of the domain are impervious, except where a boundary is activated.

  • A bottom above the top is refused. So is a bottom that equals it, and a raster that leaves one cell without a value.

  • Layers cannot pinch out. They share one rule of proportions, and the thickness cannot fall below \(H_\mathrm{min}\) with a raster kind.

  • Boussinesq has one layer and no \(S_s\). It reads \(K\) and \(S_y\) only.

  • Newton and rewetting exclude each other in MODFLOW 6.

  • A steady first period is refused with the soil reservoir. The reservoir sends a daily recharge that is zero after a dry day, and a one-period steady state would start the run from a drained aquifer.

  • Each solver has its own boundaries. What each solver supports is listed in Solvers, and the file is refused when the solver cannot carry a boundary it asks for.

Where the one-layer reading stops#

A model with one layer, and the Boussinesq solver always, are Dupuit by construction: the flow is horizontal and the head does not vary with depth [Brutsaert, 2005, Dupuit, 1863]. Over a flow path of length \(L_h\) through a saturated thickness \(B\), the vertical head difference against the horizontal one scales as \(\beta\,(B / L_h)^2\), an order of magnitude without prefactor. With \(B = 30\) m and \(\beta = 10\), that is 0.036 under hillslopes of 500 m and 0.9 under hillslopes of 100 m, where Dupuit no longer holds. Within about \(B\sqrt{\beta}\) of a stream (100 m here, one or two cells of a 75 m grid), the flow converges and bends upwards. A one-layer model leaves that resistance to the conductance of the drain or of the stream cell (How water leaves the aquifer).

The water table follows the topography, and the drains carry much of the outflow, when the criterion of Haitjema and Mitchell-Bruker [2005] exceeds 1. It compares the recharge, times the squared distance between streams, with the conductivity, the thickness and the largest rise of the land above the streams, and it assumes a uniform recharge and thickness. Below 1, the drains act along the streams only.

HydroModPy has no seepage face below the top of a cell. The drains of the top layer and the surface closures of the Boussinesq solver take the water at the cell top, and a stream imposed as a head fixes the head of the whole cell. With modflow6.sgrid.vertical.nlay above 1 and a ratio modflow6.process_specific.vka, MODFLOW 6 and MODFLOW-NWT carry one head per layer, so the vertical resistance enters the solution. The code computes none of these ratios and refuses no model for its aspect ratio. A depth factor ((15)) shortens the effective thickness.
