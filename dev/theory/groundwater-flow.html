The groundwater flow problem#

What head does the aquifer hold, in every cell and at every time? This page gives the geometry HydroModPy builds for that head, the properties that enter it and the state it starts from. The flow equation itself is MODFLOW’s and is read at its source. For the same subject in plain words, see What HydroModPy does. What each solver supports is listed in Solvers.

Note

Scope. Three solvers read the same [flow] section: MODFLOW 6 (the default), MODFLOW-NWT and the in-house Boussinesq solver. They solve the same physical problem and not the same discrete equations. The balance of the two MODFLOW solvers is in What the solvers solve. The Boussinesq form is on Boussinesq theory. What enters and leaves the aquifer (recharge, drains, streams, lakes, wells) has its own pages: Rain and evaporation: the forcing, The daily soil reservoir and How water leaves the aquifer. Where the one-layer reading stops being true is at the end of Limits.

The domain#

Two surfaces bound the aquifer.

The top \(z_\mathrm{top}\) is the digital elevation model of the catchment, read from data.dem. By default the grid takes the value of the DEM at the center of each cell, and nothing else moves the top. solver.modflow6.sgrid.top_sampling.mode can ask for a statistic over the pixels of the cell instead, with a lower value for the cells that hold a channel (6. The top of a cell).

The bottom \(z_\mathrm{bot}\) comes from a depth model, chosen by domain.depth_model.kind. Four kinds exist:

A flat substratum is an elevation, not a depth: where the land surface drops under it, no aquifer is left. A raster kind:

  1. reprojects the raster onto the grid of the top;

  2. refuses the run if one cell of the domain has no raster value, so the raster must cover the whole extent of the domain, buffer included when geographic.domain_extent is "box";

  3. applies \(\lambda\) and \(\zeta\);

  4. lowers the bottom to \(z_\mathrm{top} - H_\mathrm{min}\) wherever it would come closer to the top than that, and logs a warning that counts the cells;

  5. refuses the run if every cell needs that correction, since no aquifer would remain.

Take a top of 120 m everywhere and a raster that holds 40 m, or 85 m for the elevation kind, in every cell but one. The table gives the bottom of that cell, as build_domain returns it (the check ran the function).

The solver grid and its layers#

The grid samples \(z_\mathrm{top}\) and \(z_\mathrm{bot}\) at the center of each cell (the cell layout is on The mesh). A cell with no finite value in either surface becomes inactive in every layer. The grid is refused when \(z_\mathrm{bot} \ge z_\mathrm{top}\) on a valid cell. On a triangle or Voronoi mesh, a warning names the columns thicker than 500 m and than 10 times the median: a raster nodata value read as an elevation produces them (9. The checks the code applies).

The thickness is then cut into \(n_\mathrm{lay}\) layers. The bottom of layer \(j\) is

(16)#\[z_{\mathrm{bot},j} = z_\mathrm{top} - H\,p_j , \qquad p_{n_\mathrm{lay}} = 1 .\]

The shares \(p_j\) are the same for every cell. Three rules build them, chosen by domain.layers.genmtd_lay:

genmtd_lay

\(p_j\)

constant

\(j / n_\mathrm{lay}\)

decay, with \(\rho\) read from domain.layers.lay_decay

\((1 - \rho^{j}) / (1 - \rho^{n_\mathrm{lay}})\)

list

the running sum of domain.layers.lay_proportions

The decay rule needs \(\rho > 1\), and the file is refused otherwise: the layers then thicken with depth. With \(\rho = 2\) and four layers they hold 1, 2, 4 and 8 fifteenths of the column, from the top down. A list must sum to 1. With one layer, \(p_1 = 1\) and the column is one cell. MODFLOW 6 and MODFLOW-NWT both read the table [domain.layers], so a project has one layering whatever solver runs it. A fourth value, surfaces, gives the base of each layer instead of a share (Layers by surfaces).

What the layers can represent. Every layer reuses the planar cells and their connections. With shares, only the top and the bottom of the column vary from cell to cell, and geology varies through the properties of each layer and cell (The properties). With surfaces, the base of each layer varies too, and a layer thinner than its minimum thickness is pinched out: its cell becomes a vertical pass-through cell, idomain = -1, or keeps the minimum thickness (Layers by surfaces). A pass-through cell holds no head, and MODFLOW 6 connects the cells above and below it. A change of the plan from one layer to the next, or a connection that is not the one of the plan, is not represented. MODFLOW 6 builds every grid, structured or not, as a vertex grid (DISV), never as DISU. The Boussinesq solver has no layers: it works on one triangle per column. In the code: layer_proportions, layer_stack.

A depth factor. A property can carry a field_vertical_profile. The value of layer \(j\) in a column is the value of the field times \(f(d_j)\), where \(d_j\) is the depth of the middle of the layer below \(z_\mathrm{top}\). For kind = "exponential":

(17)#\[f(d) = e^{-d/d_c}, \qquad \text{or } \max\bigl(e^{-d/d_c},\ f_\mathrm{min}\bigr) \text{ when } f_\mathrm{min} \text{ is set.}\]

Without min_factor, the factor has no floor and tends to zero with depth. For kind = "tabulated", the factor is a linear or stepwise interpolation of the table you give, equal to the first and last factor outside the table. With one layer, \(d_j\) is half the thickness: the profile then gives one factor per cell and no variation with depth. A profile on fewer than five layers is warned about when the file is read. Take a column of thickness \(H = 2.5\,d_c\) and \(K\) at the middle of each equal layer. One layer gives a transmissivity off by \(-22\,\%\) to \(+75\,\%\) as the water table goes from the top to mid-column, three layers by \(-2.8\,\%\) at the top, and five layers by \(-1.0\,\%\).

Vertical conductivity. MODFLOW 6 reads \(K_z = K / \beta\), with \(\beta\) the solver.modflow6.process_specific.vka, layer by layer, so a depth factor on \(K\) reaches \(K_z\). A parameter with the id Kv replaces it with a conductivity per cell, and a file that gives Kv and a vka other than 1 is refused. MODFLOW-NWT reads the same Kv as the vertical conductivity of UPW: the code writes layvka = 0 and vka = Kv, converted to the time unit of the solver like \(K\). A Kv that is not positive is refused.

The code refuses a \(K\) that is zero, negative or not finite on an active cell. A heterogeneous \(K\) whose zones do not cover the whole domain produces exactly that. In the code: FieldParam.vertical_factor.

The conductivity tensor#

The NPF package of MODFLOW 6 reads the tensor by its principal values, \(K\) along the first axis, K22 along the second and \(K_z\) along the third, and by three angles [Langevin et al., 2017]. Four parameters complete \(K\) and Kv for it. K22 is the conductivity along the second axis, and MODFLOW 6 takes \(K\) when it is left out. angle1 turns the first axis from east towards north, counter-clockwise, so it is not a compass azimuth and N30E reads angle1 = 60. angle2 raises the first axis above the horizontal, and angle3 turns the tensor about it. All three are in degrees, zero when they are left out. Each is a [flow.param.<id>] like \(K\), homogeneous or per zone. K22 takes a vertical profile like \(K\), an angle does not. The parameters reach NPF only when the flow sets them, so a run without them writes the same files as before. A non-zero angle2 or angle3 turns XT3D on, because MODFLOW 6 reads these two angles only under XT3D, and so does a K22 whose axes leave the connections of the mesh (XT3D on irregular meshes). The file is refused with MODFLOW-NWT or Boussinesq, which would drop them, and with an angle2 outside \([-90, 90]\) degrees. The run store keeps \(K_z\) and, when set, K22 and the three angles with the other static properties (Storage layout).

What the solvers solve#

MODFLOW 6 and MODFLOW-NWT write Darcy’s law and mass conservation for the head in each cell, by control volumes, with one head per cell at its center [Harbaugh, 2005, Langevin et al., 2017]; chapter 2 of Langevin et al. [2017] gives the control-volume form. The derivation is not repeated here. Three pieces of it are used by other pages of this site, so they are written once.

In a catchment the aquifer is mostly unconfined. The head can sit below the top of a cell, and then the thickness that carries flow is the saturated thickness

(18)#\[B(h) = \min\bigl(\max(h - z_\mathrm{bot},\ 0),\ H\bigr) .\]

For a cell \(n\), the balance of one time step is

(19)#\[\sum_{m \in \mathcal{N}(n)} G_{nm}\,(h_m - h_n) + \sum_{p} \Phi_{n,p} = \frac{1}{\delta t}\Bigl[\,S_s\,a_n\,B_n\,(h_n - h_n^\mathrm{old}) + S_y\,a_n\,(B_n - B_n^\mathrm{old})\Bigr] .\]

The left side is the water that enters the cell during the step: the exchange with the neighbors and the stress packages. The right side is the change of the volume stored. \(h^\mathrm{old}\) is the head at the start of the step. This is the one statement of the balance on the site; the solver pages refer to it. Between two cells of a layer, the conductance joins the two half cells in series:

(20)#\[\frac{1}{G_{nm}} = \frac{c_{nm}}{K_n\,B_n\,w_{nm}} + \frac{c_{mn}}{K_m\,B_m\,w_{nm}} ,\]

with \(w_{nm}\) the width of the shared face and \(c_{nm}\) the distance from the center of \(n\) to that face. Under the Newton formulation, the series is built with the full cell thicknesses and multiplied by the saturated fraction of the upstream cell ([Langevin et al., 2017]).

What HydroModPy sets:

  • Every cell is convertible. icelltype = 1 in the MODFLOW 6 NPF package, iconvert = 1 in STO, laytyp = 1 in the MODFLOW-NWT UPW package. The specific yield \(S_y\) acts while the water table is inside the cell, and \(S_s\) above the top of a full cell. In example 04, \(S_y = 0.05\) and \(S_s B \le 3 \times 10^{-4}\), a ratio of about 170.

  • No CELL_AVERAGING option. The series average of the equation above is the MODFLOW 6 default.

  • Newton, with under-relaxation, by default. MODFLOW-NWT is the Newton formulation of MODFLOW-2005 [Niswonger et al., 2011], on the same convertible layers and a structured grid. The options and the stopping tests are on The solution: Newton and IMS.

  • Voronoi faces bisect the centers. That is the case the two-point form of (20) needs. Elsewhere, the XT3D correction is on XT3D on irregular meshes.

  • The Boussinesq solver writes the same balance on triangles. Its edge flux takes the harmonic mean of \(K\) and the arithmetic mean of \(B\) of the two cells, and its storage has no \(S_s\). See Boussinesq theory.

The smoothing of \(B\) near the bottom of a cell is on The solvers of the aquifer.

Time#

The run is cut into stress periods of length \(\Delta t\), one per forcing value (Rain and evaporation: the forcing). Within a period the forcing is constant. Each period is cut into \(n_\mathrm{sub}\) equal time steps \(\delta t = \Delta t / n_\mathrm{sub}\), and the time integration is the backward Euler scheme of [Langevin et al., 2017]: (19) is written with the stress and the conductances at the end of the step. The time step multiplier is 1, so the steps of a period are equal. The one exception is solver.modflow6.runtime.mf6_ats: with it, MODFLOW 6 may cut a transient step further where the solver does not converge, never above the step asked for. The Boussinesq solver takes one step per period.

The solver time step is a numerical choice: a period solved in one step differs from the same period in several. Time steps gives the size of that difference on a test case.

flow.flow_regime picks the regime. A steady run is one steady period over the whole [simulation.time] window, whatever step the file writes. In a transient run, flow.first_period_steady marks the first period as steady, in the three solvers. In a steady period the right side of the balance is zero, the storage plays no part, and the heads settle where the forcing of that period puts them. Example 04 states the regime:

A long run in windows#

A long transient run can be cut into windows, and Long transient runs in windows gives the commands. Each window is a run of its own, sealed with its own final_state/, and window \(k\) starts from the final state of window \(k-1\). A window edge must fall on the end of a stress period, because a period cut in two would be another problem than the one the run in one piece solves.

[simulation.time]

Each window is a sealed run of its own, named after the run with _w1, _w2 and so on, that starts from the final state of the window before it. The forcings and the soil reservoir are computed over the whole simulation, so the chain gives the heads of the run in one piece. Absent, the run is one window.

A chain is meant to give the same heads as the run in one piece, and it does when the state that passes from one window to the next is not changed on the way. I measured it on a transient MODFLOW 6 model of twelve monthly periods, five time steps each, on a grid of 25 by 25 convertible cells, with a variable recharge and a constant head on one edge, cut after six periods. HydroModPy reads final_state/ in float64 and gives the head to MODFLOW 6 as a binary array of float64, so a chain of windows equals the run in one piece to the last bit. A lake stage is written with all its digits for the same reason. On a lake linked to the cells below it, a window that started again from initial_stage was 2.3e-2 m away in head and 3.6e-2 m in stage after six months, a stage written with eight decimals was 1.3e-9 m and 2.1e-9 m away, and a stage written with all its digits was equal to the bit.

One case stays short of the last bit. With SFR and its STORAGE option, final_state/ also keeps the stage of each reach and the next window gives it to MODFLOW 6 as INITIALSTAGES. Without it window 2 is 1.0e-4 m away in head and 1.0e-7 m in stage. With it the difference is 6.4e-10 m in head and 9.0e-13 m in stage. MODFLOW 6 keeps a routing state of the reach beyond its stage, and nothing reads that state again. Writing the stage with 17 digits gives the same difference, so it does not come from the text.

The starting heads of a computed start (steady_state and spinup_cyclic) are those of the run in one piece, and they apply to window 1 only. flow.restart_from is the start of window 1. The soil reservoir runs on the whole simulation, and each window takes its own periods of that run (The daily soil reservoir).

The solver does not read this table. prepare_solver turns it into one initial state of the frozen Model, and the backend builds its start heads from that state. The auxiliary solves below are two variants of the Model: steady_variant for steady_state, and cycle_variant for each cycle of spinup_cyclic. A variant is a new object, so the Model of the run is never changed.

The equilibrium. For steady_state, the code first solves an auxiliary steady problem with the same solver, the same grid and the same properties. It starts from the top, whatever the backend, and steady_variant gives it the constant recharge and a single steady period. Its recharge is a constant, set by flow.ic.h.source: the time mean of the record, each period by its length, or the rate that you state. The result is \(h_0\):

\[\nabla\!\cdot\!\bigl(K\,B(h^\star)\,\nabla h^\star\bigr) + \bar N + q_\mathrm{bc}(h^\star) = 0 , \qquad h_0 = h^\star ,\]

where \(\bar N\) is the constant recharge and \(q_\mathrm{bc}\) the exchanges with the boundaries per unit area, held at the value of the first period (flow.ic.h.boundary_condition_policy).

The settled cycle. For spinup_cyclic, the run repeats its own period. Cycle \(c + 1\) starts from the heads where cycle \(c\) ended, \(h_\mathrm{end}^{(c)}\), and the first cycle starts from flow.ic.h.first_cycle_from. cycle_variant hands the end heads of cycle \(c\) to cycle \(c + 1\) as its start. The loop stops when

(21)#\[\max_n \bigl|\,h^{(c+1)}_{\mathrm{end},n} - h^{(c)}_{\mathrm{end},n}\,\bigr| \le \varepsilon_h ,\]

with \(\varepsilon_h\) the flow.ic.h.tol_head, or after flow.ic.h.max_cycles cycles. The maximum counts every cell, not the mean.

When the first period is steady. With flow.first_period_steady at its default, period 0 is solved as a steady state, and the heads above are only the first guess of that solve. They change the convergence and not the state reached. To make the run start from a state you choose, set it to false (see [flow.ic]).

A run can also restart from the last time step of an earlier run (flow.restart_from). That state replaces \(h_0\). In the code: build_head_initial_condition_array.

Starts the file refuses. The cycle carries the heads. It does not carry the stage of a lake, which starts again from its initial_stage at each cycle, so a file with a lake and a spinup_cyclic start is refused when it is read. hmp spinup carries both from one cycle to the next. Start from its result with flow.restart_from. That key beside a steady_state or a spinup_cyclic start is refused too, because the computed start would replace the restart without a message. The auxiliary steady solve of steady_state can leave heads that did not converge. A transport run that follows such a start stops with SolverDivergedError, since particles and solutes would follow a head field that is not a solution. The soil reservoir has a start of its own, and the two starts can come from two windows. The starting state says when that is wanted.

Recharge and injection are positive when water enters the aquifer, and a well with a negative rate pumps. Where no boundary is activated, the edge of the domain carries no flow. Limits#

  • Shallow and unconfined. Every cell is convertible. The code is built for the saturated zone of a catchment, from the surface down to a substratum that you place. The thickness is chosen, not computed.

  • No unsaturated flow. The code builds no UZF package. The water that reaches the water table is a recharge, given or computed by the daily soil store of The daily soil reservoir.

  • No density-dependent flow, no compaction, no delayed yield. The code builds neither a BUY nor a CSUB package, and the specific yield releases its water at once.

  • Constant forcing, impervious edges. The forcing is constant over a stress period. The bottom and the sides of the domain are impervious, except where a boundary is activated.

  • A bottom above the top is refused. So is a bottom that equals it, and a raster that leaves one cell without a value.

  • Only layers by surfaces pinch out. Shares give every cell the same proportions, so no layer reaches zero thickness, and the thickness cannot fall below \(H_\mathrm{min}\) with a raster kind. The thickness of a layer by surfaces is an input and is not calibrable.

  • Boussinesq has one layer and no \(S_s\). It reads \(K\) and \(S_y\) only.

  • Newton and rewetting exclude each other in MODFLOW 6.

  • A transient run with the soil reservoir refuses a steady first period. The reservoir sends a daily recharge that is zero after a dry day, and a one-period steady state would start the run from a drained aquifer.

  • Each solver has its own boundaries. What each solver supports is listed in Solvers, and the file is refused when the solver cannot carry a boundary it asks for.

Where the one-layer reading stops#

A model with one layer, and the Boussinesq solver always, are Dupuit by construction: the flow is horizontal and the head does not vary with depth [Brutsaert, 2005, Dupuit, 1863]. Over a flow path of length \(L_h\) through a saturated thickness \(B\), the vertical head difference against the horizontal one scales as \(\beta\,(B / L_h)^2\), an order of magnitude without prefactor. With \(B = 30\) m and \(\beta = 10\), that is 0.036 under hillslopes of 500 m and 0.9 under hillslopes of 100 m, where Dupuit no longer holds. Within about \(B\sqrt{\beta}\) of a stream (100 m here, one or two cells of a 75 m grid), the flow converges and bends upwards. A one-layer model leaves that resistance to the conductance of the drain or of the stream cell (How water leaves the aquifer).

The water table follows the topography, and the drains carry much of the outflow, when the criterion of Haitjema and Mitchell-Bruker [2005] exceeds 1. It compares the recharge, times the squared distance between streams, with the conductivity, the thickness and the largest rise of the land above the streams, and it assumes a uniform recharge and thickness. Below 1, the drains act along the streams only.

HydroModPy has no seepage face below the top of a cell. The drains of the top layer and the surface closures of the Boussinesq solver take the water at the cell top, and a stream imposed as a head fixes the head of the whole cell. With domain.layers.nlay above 1 and a ratio solver.modflow6.process_specific.vka, MODFLOW 6 and MODFLOW-NWT carry one head per layer, so the vertical resistance enters the solution. The code computes none of these ratios and refuses no model for its aspect ratio. A depth factor ((17)) shortens the effective thickness.
