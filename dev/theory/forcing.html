Rain and evaporation: the forcing#

The aquifer model does not see days: it sees stress periods, and one rate per cell for each period. This page gives the rules that turn daily series and daily grids into those rates. How the outputs are put back on the same periods is on Time alignment of outputs and observations. The keys are in [flow.sinks_sources.recharge] and [simulation].

The period, unit, coverage and runoff rules are the same under the three solvers; sections 5 and 7 note where they differ. The daily soil reservoir (The daily soil reservoir) is here one more source of daily grids.

1. The window is cut into periods#

The edges start at \(b_0 = t_0\) and advance by one step \(\Delta t\) until the first edge that reaches the end of the last day:

(1)#\[ b_{k+1} = b_k + \Delta t, \qquad b_{n_\mathrm{per}} = t_\mathrm{end} + 1\ \text{day}\quad(+ 1\ \text{hour for an hourly step}). \]

Period \(k\) is the half-open interval \([b_k, b_{k+1})\). The end date is part of the run, so the last edge is the day after it. A step that does not land on that edge is refused: the window is not aligned with the step.

Edges can also be given explicitly, as window.period_bounds. The code sets them, and no TOML key does. flow.ic of type steady_state uses this for its auxiliary steady model: one period that spans the whole window (5. Steady periods and first_clim).

In the code: build_simulation_time_boundaries.

2. One series per variable#

The data layer hands a variable over as station records or as grids. Two rules reduce them to the shape the flow needs.

Stations. Several stations are averaged at each stamp, with equal weights. A single station is used as it is.

Spatial mode. spatial_mode decides what stays a series and what stays a grid:

Mode

Under auto, station records with coordinates become a grid only when no series can be built from them. A grid is not resampled when it is loaded: it is placed on the cells of the solver mesh once the mesh exists, by the method of interpolation_method (nearest, linear or idw). The ETP has the same two keys.

In the code: resolve_forcing.

3. Units#

Every data manager hands rain, ETP, recharge and runoff over in mm/day. The binders convert them with the fixed factor \(1/(1000 \times 86400)\) m/s per mm/day, whatever units says. That key is read for a rate written in the file: values and a numeric first_clim are converted from it to m/s when the flow is built.

A sample is a rate. The code averages samples and never sums them: a monthly total in mm must be turned into mm/day before it is given to the model.

4. A record gets one value per period#

One rule holds for every forcing record: a series, a station, or a grid with a time axis, which is averaged cell by cell of its own grid before it is placed on the mesh.

Samples inside the period. Period \(k\) takes the equal-weight mean of the \(n_k\) samples stamped inside it:

(2)#\[ \bar x_k = \frac{1}{n_k} \sum_{t_j \in [b_k,\, b_{k+1})} x_j \qquad \text{if } n_k > 0 . \]

The mean has equal weights, so it is the time mean only for a record with one sample per equal step. Several gridded records that cover one period are averaged the same way, with equal weights.

No sample inside. A sample is the rate of the interval from its stamp to the next stamp. The last sample stands for one step \(\Delta t_r\) of the record, the median spacing of its stamps, and a record of one sample stands for all later time. A period with no sample inside is covered when the interval of the latest earlier sample reaches into it, and takes that sample. So daily data on hourly periods, and a gap inside a record, are covered.

Not covered. A period that no interval reaches, before the first stamp or after the end of the last step, is not covered. coverage_policy decides: "error", the default, stops the run and names the periods; "warn" warns once per record; "ignore" says nothing. Under the last two, the period takes the nearest sample in time: the last one after the record, the first one before it.

5. Steady periods and first_clim#

A steady period stands for the long-term state a run starts from. It does not take the rate of its own dates. first_clim sets the rate \(\hat N\) of every steady stress period, and of no other period:

(3)#\[\begin{split} \hat N = \begin{cases} \dfrac{1}{n_\mathrm{per}} \displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n_\mathrm{per}-1} N_k & \texttt{"mean"},\\[1.5ex] N_0 & \texttt{"first"},\\[0.5ex] F & \text{a number } F,\ \text{in the units of the values.} \end{cases} \end{split}\]

The mean has one weight per period, whatever the length of the period. The steady periods are every period of a steady run, and period 0 of a transient run when flow.first_period_steady is true, in the three solvers. A transient period keeps its own rate, whatever its index. Under MODFLOW-NWT the ETP follows the same rule with first_clim.

6. Negative recharge goes to the ETP package#

A net recharge can be negative: a series of rain minus ETP, for example. With negative_to_evt, it is split into a recharge part and a deficit part:

(4)#\[ N^+_k = \max(N_k,\, 0), \qquad N^-_k = \max(-N_k,\, 0). \]

The recharge package receives \(N^+_k\) and the evapotranspiration package \(N^-_k\). A transient period keeps its own split. A steady period takes first_clim on each part: "mean" gives the mean of \(N^+_k\) and the mean of \(N^-_k\), "first" gives \(N^+_0\) and \(N^-_0\), and a number \(F\) gives \(\max(F, 0)\) and \(\max(-F, 0)\). So the net rate of a steady period, \(\hat N^+ - \hat N^-\), is always the one first_clim states: the mean of the net rates, \(N_0\), or \(F\).

A negative number for first_clim needs negative_to_evt, or the file is refused. With negative_to_evt off, a negative rate in the file is refused when it is read. The deficit is drawn from the water table near the top of each cell, down to the extinction depth \(d_\mathrm{ext}\), and not under the sea, a river head or a lake (Drains and evapotranspiration at the top of the model).

In the code: split_net_recharge.

7. What the solver does with N and E#

The recharge package adds \(N_k\) times the cell area to each cell, and the evapotranspiration package removes at most \(E_k\), less as the head falls to the extinction depth. Both are constant during a stress period. The packages are documented by MODFLOW: RCHA and EVTA in MODFLOW 6 ([Langevin et al., 2017]), RCH and EVT in MODFLOW-2005 and MODFLOW-NWT ([Harbaugh, 2005, Niswonger et al., 2011]). What HydroModPy decides is which rate reaches the ETP package:

  • MODFLOW-NWT. The loaded ETP drives the package, with the deficit of 6. Negative recharge goes to the ETP package.

  • MODFLOW 6 without a soil reservoir. The loaded ETP is ignored, with a warning if "etp" is in flow.active_sinks_sources. Only the deficit reaches the package.

  • MODFLOW 6 with a soil reservoir. The ETP the soil could not meet reaches the package.

  • Boussinesq. "etp" in flow.active_sinks_sources is refused.

Under MODFLOW 6 the recharge is set to zero on lake cells: the lake gets its own rain through the lake package. The law, the surface and the depth of the ETP package are on Drains and evapotranspiration at the top of the model.

8. Runoff is not recharge#

The runoff of the data layer, data.runoff, never enters the aquifer as a diffuse term. It enters the model, when it enters at all, as a volume per time:

(5)#\[ Q^\mathrm{ro}_k = \bar q^\mathrm{ro}_k\,A , \]

where \(\bar q^\mathrm{ro}_k\) is the catchment mean of the runoff, in m/s, put on the periods by the rule of 4. A record gets one value per period, and \(A\) the catchment area.

  • With an active stream network, \(Q^\mathrm{ro}_k\) is shared among the reaches by their length and routed downstream. A forcing written in the file wins over the loaded series, and a lake then gets no direct share, so the same water is not counted twice (Streams and the water mover).

  • Without a network, a declared lake receives \(Q^\mathrm{ro}_k\) directly (Lakes and reservoirs).

  • Without either, the runoff stays outside the flow.

A lake also gets the catchment mean of the loaded rain and ETP as open-water rates, unless it declares its own.

When a calibration compares a simulated drain budget with a gauge, the runoff is added to the simulated discharge, averaged on each period and multiplied by the catchment area, or by the area the gauge drains (Time alignment of outputs and observations). If a stream network already carries the runoff, nothing is added.

9. The soil reservoir as a source of grids#

With a soil reservoir (flow.sinks_sources.recharge.soil), the data layer loads rain and ETP, and the reservoir computes three daily grids in mm/day: recharge, the ETP it could not meet, and runoff. The binders take them where they would take a loaded recharge, ETP and runoff, so they go through the rules of 4. A record gets one value per period and 8. Runoff is not recharge. A daily step gives one slice per period; a monthly step gives the mean of the daily slices of the month.

A soil run refuses a steady first period, since that period would hold the recharge of one day only. A soil run therefore has no steady period, and first_clim acts on none of its periods. The daily rules are on The daily soil reservoir.

Assumptions and limits#

  • Constant within the period. Rain, ETP and runoff are one value per period and per cell. Recharge is a flux set from outside and does not read the water table.

  • "mean" weights each period equally. On periods of unequal length, the steady rate is not the time mean of the record.

  • The ETP is not read under MODFLOW 6 outside soil mode. A loaded ETP is ignored, with a warning.

  • Aggregation by last exists for declared forcings only. The data layer always takes the mean.
