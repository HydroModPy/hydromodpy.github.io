Glossary#

The terms used in this part of the site, with a short definition. Where a term has a setting in the configuration file, the second line of its entry says where. A term can be cited from another page with {term}`unconfined aquifer`.

On the pages of this part, a term underlined with dots shows its definition when you point at it or move the keyboard focus to it. On a touch screen, tap it once for the definition and a second time to open this page.

actual evapotranspiration#

The part of the potential evapotranspiration that is really taken, limited by the water available in the soil. Abbreviated AET. The soil reservoir computes it.

aquifer#

Rock or sediment saturated with water, which lets the water move and stores it.

aquifer thickness#

Depth of the modeled layer below the ground surface: 30 m in example 04. This is an assumption. The depth to water table cannot exceed it.

In the file: domain.depth_model.thickness.

available water capacity#

Water that roots can take from the soil, in mm. Abbreviated AWC. It sets how much water the soil keeps before it lets water pass to the water table.

In the file: flow.sinks_sources.recharge.soil.ru.

baseflow#

The part of the river flow that comes from the aquifer. In the model: the water that leaves the aquifer through the drains, summed over the catchment.

BD Topage#

The reference hydrographic network of France, published by Sandre. HydroModPy can download it as a mapped network. It is not the same product as the BD TOPO.

In the file: data.hydrography.

BD TOPO#

The topographic database of the French national mapping agency (IGN). Example 04 takes its mapped network from the permanent reaches of this database.

In the file: data.hydrography.

calibration#

Tuning of the model parameters (here K, then Sy) so that its results look like observations. A calibrated result is not, for that reason, validated (see validation).

In the file: calibration.protocol.name.

catchment#

Surface that gathers water toward a single exit point, the outlet. In HydroModPy, it is drawn from the relief and the position of the outlet.

In the file: geographic.catchment.catch_def.

cell#

One square of the computation grid: 75 m wide in example 04. The model computes one value per cell, and the maps count cells. The set of cells that cuts the model area is the mesh.

conductance#

How freely a drain passes water for a given rise of the water table. Unless you declare one, the model derives it from the hydraulic conductivity and from the thickness of the clogging layer of the drain bed.

In the file: solver.drain_bed_thickness_m.

criterion#

A number that a calibration drives toward a target, over many trials. It must behave well along that search. The same number, only read, is a diagnostic.

depth to water table#

Distance between the ground and the top of the water table, in meters, never negative.

Figure: watertable_depth_map, in display.figures.

diagnostic#

A number that you read to describe a result, put in a report or use to compare two runs. It is not driven to a target. See criterion.

digital elevation model#

Map of the ground elevation, on a regular grid. Abbreviated DEM.

In the file: data.dem.

downslope distance#

Distance a cell travels along the slope before it reaches a network. Written \(D_{so}\) from the simulated network to the mapped one, and \(D_{os}\) in the opposite direction. It measures how far apart two networks are.

drain#

Condition that lets water leave the aquifer when the water table reaches its elevation, never the reverse. In example 04, the drains are placed at the ground surface and their capacity (the conductance) is deduced from K.

In the file: flow.active_bc.

ensemble#

A set of runs of the same model with different parameters or inputs. The spread between the runs shows how much a result depends on those choices. See How sure is the model?.

equifinality#

Several different sets of parameters reproduce the observations about equally well. The calibration then cannot say which set is right. See How sure is the model?.

excess#

A cell where the model puts a stream and the map has none. See valid.

forcing#

What drives the model through time: the recharge, or the rainfall and the potential evapotranspiration from which a soil reservoir computes it.

GR4J#

A rainfall-runoff model that treats a catchment as a single unit. HydroModPy can calibrate it in place of the distributed model.

groundwater#

Water that fills the pores of the ground below the water table.

Hub’Eau#

The public interface to French water data. HydroModPy can download the discharge measured at a gauging station from its hydrometry service.

In the file: data.hydrometry.

hydraulic conductivity#

How easily the subsurface lets water pass, written K, in m/s. It cannot be measured at the scale of a catchment: it is calibrated.

In the file: flow.param.K.field.value.

hydraulic head#

Elevation of the water table at a point. It is the unknown that the model computes in each cell; the depth to water table is deduced from it.

In the file (initial state): flow.ic.h.type.

hydrograph#

Curve of the flow as a function of time.

indicative#

A reliability label. No field measurement was compared with this result. See reliability label.

initial condition#

State of the water table at the start of the computation. In example 04, a first month in steady state provides it.

In the file: flow.ic.h.type, flow.first_period_steady.

interfluve#

The higher ground between two valleys. The water table is deep under it.

intermittent flow#

A stream that flows for only part of the year. On the intermittence map: a cell that flows at some time steps of the year, not at all of them.

lockfile#

The file hydromodpy.lock at the root of a project. It pins the input data by checksum and records the version of the code and of the solver binary. See Can I check what was run?.

mapped network#

Streams drawn on a map (here, the permanent reaches of the BD TOPO). It is a reference to compare and calibrate against, not an input to the flow computation.

In the file: data.hydrography.

mesh#

The set of cells that cuts the model area. The word grid means the same thing when the cells are squares.

missing#

A cell where the map has a stream and the model puts none. See valid.

MODFLOW 6#

The groundwater flow code of the United States Geological Survey (USGS). It is the solver of example 04. HydroModPy also runs MODFLOW-NWT, an older code of the same family, and a Boussinesq solver written for HydroModPy.

NANCON station#

The gauge at the outlet of the Nançon catchment. Example 04 compares the discharge measured there with the simulated one.

In the file: data.hydrometry.

NSE#

Nash-Sutcliffe efficiency: compares simulated and measured flow. 1 is a perfect match, 0 is the same as predicting the mean of the measurements, and a negative value is worse. The “log” variant computes it on the logarithms to give weight to low flows.

In the file: calibration.protocol.transient_metric.

numerical check#

A reliability label. An internal check of the computation, independent of the calibration. See reliability label.

objective#

What a calibration tries to reach: one or several criteria, each computed on an observation such as the stream network or the discharge.

In the file: calibration.objective_blocks.metric.

outlet#

Exit point of the catchment, where the flow is measured.

In the file: geographic.catchment.x_outlet.

percent bias#

Volume gap between the simulated flow and the measured flow, as a percentage of the measured volume. Abbreviated PBIAS. It is positive when the model underestimates, that is when it is too dry, and negative when it overestimates.

perennial flow#

A stream that flows all year. On the intermittence map: a cell that flows at every time step of the year.

persistence#

Share of the time steps during which a cell flows, over the whole simulation.

potential evapotranspiration#

Amount of water that the atmosphere could take back from a soil that is well supplied with water. Abbreviated PET.

In the file: data.etp.

provenance#

The record of what produced a result: the version of the code, the solver, the input files and their checksums. See Can I check what was run?.

reach#

A stretch of stream between two points.

reanalysis#

A gridded estimate of past weather or hydrology, made by running a model over all the observations available. SIM2 is one.

recharge#

Water that reaches the water table from the soil. It is supplied, or computed by a soil reservoir.

In the file: flow.sinks_sources.recharge, data.recharge.

reliability label#

One of three words that tell how far a result can be trusted: numerical check, calibrated and indicative. They are used under every figure of Reading the results.

resolution#

The size of one cell, 75 m in example 04. A feature smaller than the resolution is not drawn.

runoff#

Water that reaches the stream without passing through the water table. In example 04, it is given to the model as data and is added to the drainage to form the simulated flow.

In the file: data.runoff.

scoring window#

Period over which the calibration compares simulated and measured flow. In example 04, 2001 and 2002; the year 2000 is used as spin-up.

In the file: calibration.protocol.scoring_window.

seepage#

Cell where the water table reaches the ground surface and where water leaves the aquifer.

Rebuilt on read from the head and the ground surface (or from the surface excess of a Boussinesq run); nothing to set.

seepage threshold#

Fraction of its own recharge that a cell must give back to count as seepage: 0.01 % by default. Another threshold changes the network maps.

In the file: calibration.outputs.<name>.tau_specific_ratio.

SIM2#

Météo-France reanalysis on an 8 km grid, which supplies rainfall and potential evapotranspiration to the soil reservoir.

In the file: data.etp.

simulated network#

Extension downstream, following the slope of the terrain, of the cells where the aquifer gives water back to the surface. It does not result from a hydraulic river computation.

soil reservoir#

Simple daily balance: the soil fills with rain, empties by evapotranspiration and lets the surplus pass to the water table.

In the file: flow.sinks_sources.recharge.soil.

specific yield#

Share of the rock volume that drains when the water table falls, written Sy, without dimension. It sets the speed at which the water table empties and refills.

In the file: flow.param.Sy.field.value.

spin-up#

Start of the simulation that is not scored, while the initial state of the model fades out. In example 04, the year 2000.

In the file: calibration.protocol.scoring_window.

spring#

A place where groundwater comes out of the ground. A seepage cell is a spring of the model.

steady state#

Computation where nothing changes with time: the recharge is a constant mean.

In the file: flow.flow_regime.

stream head#

The upstream end of a stream, where it starts to flow. The heads dry up first in summer.

stress period#

Interval over which the forcing is held constant. It is one month in example 04. The solver may cut it into smaller time steps.

In the file: simulation.time.step_value, simulation.time.substeps_per_period.

time step#

On the maps and curves of this part of the site, the unit of time of the results: one month in example 04. Strictly, the month is a stress period and the solver may cut it into smaller steps.

TOML#

The plain-text format of the configuration file. One TOML file describes a whole run. The files of example 04 are walked through in Get started.

transient#

Computation where the recharge and the water table vary from one time step to the next.

In the file: flow.flow_regime.

trial#

One run of the model with one set of parameter values, tried by a calibration. A calibration runs many trials and keeps the best.

uncertainty#

How far a result could be from the truth. See How sure is the model?.

unconfined aquifer#

Aquifer whose top is not trapped under an impermeable layer: it rises and falls freely with the recharge. HydroModPy targets this type of aquifer.

valid#

A cell where the simulated network and the mapped network both pass. With excess and missing, it gives the three classes of the confusion map.

validation#

A comparison of a model with measurements that the calibration has not seen. See What “validated” means here.

visible flow#

Minimum flow that a cell must carry to count as “flowing” on the persistence maps: 1 L/s by default. It is a convention.

In the file: calibration.outputs.<name>.extent.visible_flow.

water budget#

What enters the aquifer (recharge), what leaves it (drainage) and the change in storage. Its closure is a numerical check of the solver.

water table#

The top of the groundwater: the level below which the ground is saturated. The model computes its elevation in every cell, as the hydraulic head.
