Conventions of a run#

A run stores numbers. This page says how to read them: which date reads which period, which sign a flux has, what a station or a zone is called, and how layers and reaches are counted. The rules are the same for every solver and they do not change from one model to the next.

A date reads the period that holds it#

A run has one value per stress period, and the time stored with a value is the end of its period: the stamp. January 2000 of a monthly run is stamped 2000-02-01.

A date t reads the period (s, e] that holds it, where s is the start of the period and e its stamp. So a date equal to a stamp reads the period that stamp closes, and a date copied from the time axis of a NetCDF file or from a CSV finds its own value again. The first date of the record belongs to the first period.

A state, such as a head, is the value at the stamp. A flux, such as a drain flux, is the mean over the period. So a date inside a period reads the state at the end of that period. On a daily run, time = "2002-10-15" reads the period that ends at the start of 15 October, which is the day of 14 October.

A window from start to end keeps every period with some time between the two dates. Two stamps keep the periods between them, so 2001-01-01 to 2002-01-01 is the twelve months of 2001 on a monthly run. A window of one date reads the period that date reads. A window that only touches the record holds no period and is refused: it starts on the last stamp, or ends on the first date.

A window over a series keeps what falls in those periods. A simulated value is kept by its stamp, (start, end]. A measurement is kept by its date, [start, end). So 2000-02-01 to 2000-03-01 is the one simulated value of February and the 29 daily measurements of February. A measurement taken before or after the simulated record is read with the whole series, without a window.

A list of dates reads one period per date. A list where two dates read the same period is refused, and the message names them: 2000-01-01 and 2000-02-01 both read January on a monthly run. To read every period between two dates, write a window.

The rule is the same in Run.read, run.field, run.timeseries, [[export]], [display] and the time of a network output of a calibration. How a run stamps its periods is on Time alignment of outputs and observations.

A budget flux is negative when water leaves the aquifer#

Every per-cell flux of the budget/ group of a run follows the sign of MODFLOW, for every solver: positive when water enters the aquifer, negative when it leaves. A drain flux is negative. A recharge is positive. A storage term is positive when the aquifer takes water from its storage. A well is negative when it pumps. A transient run of every solver writes its storage terms. Summed over the whole model domain, the signed fluxes add up to zero. This does not hold on one cell, because the flows between cells are not in budget/.

The fields rebuilt from these fluxes say what they hold in their name: outflow_drain, release_flux and the accumulation fluxes are positive outflows. The budgets table splits each term in flux_in and flux_out, both positive. In a budget table, flux_in is the water that enters the store named by zone_id: the aquifer for DOMAIN, the stream network for sfr:<id>, the lake for its station. The exchange between two stores is therefore read with the sign of the store its row names.

A rate in a TOML file follows the same sign, or its name says the direction#

A rate you write in a file has one of two forms, whatever the table.

A signed rate follows the rule of the budget: positive when water enters the store it acts on, negative when it leaves. These are the rate of a well, flow.sinks_sources.wells.<name>.flux and its forcing, the recharge, flow.sinks_sources.recharge.values, and the rate of a specified lake outlet, flow.sinks_sources.lakes.<name>.outlets.rate, where the store is the lake.

A rate whose name says its direction is a magnitude, zero or more, and a negative value is refused. These are the ETP values, and on a lake or a stream network rainfall, evaporation, runoff, inflow, withdrawal and headwater_inflow. So a withdrawal of 2 m3/s takes 2 m3/s out of the lake, and a well that takes as much says -2.

Water that leaves is written one of two ways. In the budget/ group, and in a rate that is signed (a well, a recharge, a specified lake outlet), it is negative. In a rate whose name says the direction (evaporation, withdrawal, the ETP), it is a positive magnitude.

One word for the whole catchment#

catchment means the whole catchment wherever a table needs a word for it: the station of a catchment series such as discharge, the zone of a budget row, the zone of a uniform parameter, and the soil column of the basin mean. domain is the zone of a budget row over the whole model domain. The two differ when the mesh takes in a buffer around the catchment, so only the catchment row compares with a gauge at the outlet.

A station or a zone you name cannot be called catchment, in a file or in a TOML key. A gauge with this name would be scored against the simulated outlet, and a zone would read as a uniform parameter. Write the gauge code, such as J0014010, or name the zone after what it holds, such as core or hillslope. domain stays free: only a budget row reads it, and catchment_zones uses it as a zone label.

A column says where a series comes from#

The source column of the timeseries table says simulated or observed. A simulated discharge and a gauged one have the same variable, discharge. To read the gauge by name, Run.read and [[export]] take discharge_obs, which no table stores. A CSV export has the same source column.

A reach keeps one number between the TOML and the results. Its reach_id, <network>:<reach>, is its station_id in the timeseries table, and the table sfr_reaches gives its geometry and the reaches it drains into.

A NetCDF variable or a VTU cell array keeps its name at any number of layers. A VTU file has one component per layer. In a NetCDF file a field of a model of several layers has a layer dimension, and a field of a model of one layer holds that layer alone, with the scalar variable layer naming it, because QGIS leaves out of a mesh any field with a third dimension. QGIS lists a NetCDF export three times, as a raster, a mesh and a vector. Open the mesh: a raster of a field on the cells is one row high. On a model of several layers QGIS reads a file with one layer asked for, hmp export with --layer or layer = in [[export]].

Units#

The unit column of a table, and the units attribute of a field, are written in udunits spelling: m3 s-1, mm d-1, m, 1 for a dimensionless value. A text that is not plainly a unit stays as its provider wrote it, such as mg/L NO3 or n/a. The columns of the soil_budget table hold water depths in the unit its unit column says, mm.
